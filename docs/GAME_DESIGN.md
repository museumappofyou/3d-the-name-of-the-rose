# Game direction and the next playable proof

**Build a first-person investigation game in one consistent abbey: learn how a place works, then use that knowledge to judge what happened there.** Authored original cases are the primary playable format. Optional novel-derived episodes and free study preserve the literary foundation without requiring a full plot reenactment. This is the recommended direction, not a description of already implemented gameplay.

## The player experience

You arrive as an invited reader/collator with limited authority and a reason to handle documents. A missing leaf, altered account or disputed recollection gives you a concrete question. You walk the cloister, listen to an office beginning, notice who can cross a threshold, read two apparently compatible entries and realize they describe different things. You return to a room at another hour, ask a more precise question and test whether someone’s route fits the time they gave. Your reward is a better explanation and new access, not a glowing waypoint.

Over a two-to-four-hour case, the work should alternate between orientation, observation, comparison, a provisional account, an experiment and a supported conclusion. The abbey gradually becomes legible: which window overlooks a path, which door closes for an office, how long a stair takes, where voices carry, who normally carries books and which titles hide in a catalogue. A familiar corridor can mean something different in the next case.

The player is not an omniscient detective. People can be mistaken, guarded or truthful about only part of an event. Finding an object establishes its location; it does not establish who put it there or why. Some conclusions remain uncertain. The game must distinguish a supported explanation from a confession, and both from an accusation.

The initial role avoids automatically turning the player into William or Adso. Their story and intellectual world matter, but original cases need agency the player can actually exercise. Routine chores may teach a clue-bearing practice; they must not become daily filler or a monastery-management job unless a later product decision chooses that direction.

## Seriously different product directions

| Direction | Player fantasy / loop | Case structure and novel relationship | Replayability | Complexity, current reuse and principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Literary journey** | Witness selected situations; explore/read/recognize connections | Fixed episodes accompanying the novel, with limited branch interpretation | Revisit readings, viewpoints, omitted evidence; little surprise after knowing the plot | Medium content/animation cost. Reuses discovery, locations/hours and existing cast. Distinctive faithful spatial reading; risks spoilers, passive following and agency incompatible with canonical outcomes |
| **Authored abbey investigations** | Understand an institution by investigating objects, testimony and movement | Several finite original cases overlay one abbey; novel episodes are optional | New cases and knowledge/access roles; stable truths with optional paths | Medium-to-high authoring and logic work, bounded systemic scope. Reuses all native proof systems. Distinctive physical and textual reasoning; risks brittle clue graphs or excessive reading without useful experiments |
| **A monastery’s custodian** | Keep the community functioning; allocate access/resources, resolve conflicts | Persistent days and consequences create institutional stories; historically reconstructed world around literary architecture | High through systemic consequences and changing duties | Very high simulation/content/UI scope. Hours/access help, but current crowd is not sufficient AI. Distinctive institutional responsibility; risks busywork, generic survival/management and losing the investigative focus |
| **Manuscript field school** | Discover how books and buildings preserve knowledge; inspect/collate/annotate | Self-contained scholarly problems, optional guided readings, no main suspect | New document collections and contrasting reconstructions | Moderate object/reading-tool cost; low AI demand. Reuses evidence, notebook, architecture and inspection. Distinctive accessible intellectual history; risks becoming a museum with quizzes rather than sustained play |
| **Constraint-driven mystery workshop** | Solve a different situation each visit | Authored event grammars choose people/routes/documents while preserving a coherent hidden account | Potentially high after repetition-proof validation | Very high generator/solver/test cost; current IDs/access/parity are only foundations. Distinctive reusable physical cases; risks interchangeable suspects, contradictions, unfair clues and expensive assurance work |

**Recommendation: authored investigations, with the field-school approach to documents and a separate literary/study mode.** This uses the reconstructed place as a reasoning instrument and permits small cases before a huge simulation. Do not build the custodian simulator or mystery generator now. An investigation must stand on its own as a compelling authored work before variability becomes valuable.

## Scenario portfolio and the novel

The novel should **not** be the only playable scenario. Its architecture, constrained relationships, atmosphere, rhythms, debates, material details and ways of seeking knowledge remain foundational. Changing scenario content does not give permission to discard those constraints.

| Mode | Promise | Spoiler policy |
|---|---|---|
| Original investigations | A complete new problem with original people/events in the reconstructed setting | Default; no novel murder solution, secret-route reveal or ending dependency |
| Novel-derived episodes | Examine selected documented situations, routes or knowledge structures | Clearly named opt-in episodes, with specific spoiler level and chapter range |
| Free exploration/study | Visit architecture, routines, objects and supporting evidence without a case | Public reconstruction by default; hidden routes, full library key and later novel events opt-in |
| Future authored packs | Manuscript, economic, architectural or political investigations using existing spaces differently | Pack declares era, assumed knowledge, world version and spoiler scope |

Original cases occupy their own declared day/continuity, rather than pretending new events occurred inside the novel’s seven days. Keep novel characters optional in those cases; a named monk from the novel is not a blank interchangeable suspect. The catastrophe/late-state architecture must be an episode overlay, not the default monastery for every visit.

Novel episodes should begin with one independently playable situation, not a complete adaptation. The present altar proof already exposes a novel-derived secret; it is a technical route and must not silently become the default onboarding case. The current browser reference similarly exposes secrets. Spoiler-safe products require a new mode boundary, not just a warning buried in credits.

## Strong original cases

These are proposed fiction. Historical practices can support their plausibility; none is a newly extracted novel event.

| Case | Central question and twist in reasoning | How the abbey participates | Cost / suitability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Leaf Before Vespers** | A loaned leaf is missing. A sighting of a wrapped book becomes an accusation, but possession, identity and timing do not coincide. Recovering it is easier than proving what the witness can know | Cloister/church routes, one sight line, office time, temporary access and a reading stand | Best first slice: public areas already migrated, three original people, three short documents |
| **The Rent That Was Paid Twice** | Two apparently original receipts describe one payment. A renamed holding and copied date conceal which obligation was discharged; the initial “forger” explanation is too simple | Grain storage, cellarer’s routes, visitors at the gate, public tally versus private register | Strong later institution case; economy and donor pressure, no obligatory murder |
| **The Wall That Remembers** | A building account pays for stonework nobody admits exists. Window spacing and a remembered acoustic path locate an earlier doorway; a concealed repair protects an embarrassing institutional claim | Measured thickness, line of sight, vault junctions and a forgotten maintenance route | Architectural investigation; requires one authored alteration overlay, not a new underground world |
| **The Second Hand** | A text attributed to a celebrated scholar contains a marginal argument from a different period. Is the body text forged, later annotated or copied from an older erased leaf? | Scriptorium light, catalogue chains, access to comparison exemplars and custody of a repair bundle | Manuscript scholarship; few actors, richer readable objects; avoid instant ultraviolet/magic reveal |
| **The Feast on the Wrong Day** | A commemorative entry, burial record and visitor’s letter disagree about a date. The apparent alibi changes when a liturgical label and a copied civil date are separated | Bells, cemetery access, calendrical documents and a procession witnessed from different windows | Historically grounded chronology case; explain the particular calendar convention rather than assuming universal practice |
| **The Light in the East Window** | A night light is called illicit signalling. The window can illuminate several paths, while a translated astronomical note provides another plausible purpose | Observatory sight lines, travel time, weather and illumination from a specific room | Distinctive later case; retain honest ambiguity until geometry/documents discriminate explanations |

The best themes are knowledge custody, patronage, memory, translation, authority and the monastery’s material economy. Violence can have a place, but making every book a poisoned object would flatten the setting into a repetition of the novel.

Physical manuscript clues have real precedents: catchwords connect quires in the [Trinity College Library example](https://trinitycollegelibrarycambridge.wordpress.com/2015/04/17/catchwords/); erased/reused parchment is documented by the [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/palimpsests-the-art-of-medieval-recycling). Their use in the proposed cases is a design inference. Select a dated exemplar and record it before claiming any detail is typical of this abbey. `shared/provenance/historical-sources.json` keeps the initial references.

## A reusable scenario is an event/evidence model

A scenario overlays a stable world; it is not a list of “go here, press E” quests. The minimum design contract is:

| Primitive | Required information |
|---|---|
| Identity/header | Scenario/version, required world/data version, continuity/date, role, spoiler level, entry knowledge, completion policy |
| Starting condition | What actually happened before entry, what is still happening and what the player is told |
| People | Stable scenario IDs, templates, duties, knowledge, custody/permissions, authored statements; presentation pool never invents identity |
| Locations/routes | Existing room/portal/anchor IDs, navigable routes, travel lower bounds, visibility/acoustic paths and access windows |
| Objects/documents | Stable IDs, authored readable content, physical state/custody, provenance and inspection affordances |
| Events | Preconditions, start/end time or causal trigger, participants, physical actions, resulting state; authored past events distinguished from observed ones |
| Observations | Perceivable condition, source, place/time/modality, recording rule, what it does and does not establish |
| Claims | Speaker/document, wording, time scope, certainty and referenced event; false claims never become “known facts” automatically |
| Contradictions | Competing accounts and the observation/constraint that challenges them; unavailable evidence is not evidence of absence |
| Hypotheses | Authored candidate explanations, required links, legitimate alternatives, disconfirming evidence and unanswered questions |
| Outcomes | An explanation’s acceptance conditions and justified consequences; object recovery separate from authorship/motive/accusation |
| Persistence | Scenario/version, time, events/custody, actor route progress, access, observations, claims, player links, submitted account and unresolved state |

These are a planned contract. Existing shared schemas do not implement all these primitives. Start with one data-driven case plus a few typed domain services, not an all-purpose narrative language. Reuse the closed condition/action vocabulary and add a primitive only when the slice needs it. No arbitrary code expressions in scenario data.

A future layout might hold base world records in `shared/data`, authored overlays in `shared/scenarios/<case>/`, and native adapters that bind them to scenes. Do not create empty scenario folders now or duplicate base rooms inside each case. Saves require scenario ID/version and a migration strategy before packs ship; case changes cannot reinterpret an old saved accusation silently.

## Protect reasoning

Record **“Brother Martin says he left the leaf before Vespers”**, not “leaf left before Vespers.” An inspection records observable features, not the correct explanation. A loan slip records an author’s entry, not omniscient custody. “No one there” means no one visible at that place/time, not that the person never visited it.

The notebook has three working surfaces: sourced observations/claims, player-arranged time/relationship comparisons and a provisional account. Connecting two notes should preserve their attribution and uncertainty. It can indicate that a proposed route is inconsistent with a measured interval, but must not automatically name the culprit. The player may write a note, compare readable images or select a bounded relationship such as “this claim conflicts with this observation.” Avoid a sprawling clue-combination puzzle where every pair must be brute-forced.

A conclusion asks for a short supported account: what happened, when/by which route, which item/actor the evidence identifies, and which claim is unreliable. Offer structured phrases with optional free notes so players need not guess exact prose. Test the selected links against authored constraints. Feedback names the missing support (“you have identified the leaf, but not who moved it”) without revealing the next room. A defensible alternative can receive a partial or explicitly uncertain outcome.

At least two evidence paths should support each critical conclusion: an observable current routine plus a trustworthy contemporary entry, or physical collation plus a separate reference. They need not repeat the same answer. Optional discoveries deepen interpretation rather than locking the only solvable path. Dialogue changes when the player asks about a specific conflict; NPCs must not become dispensers of the next marker.

Navigation itself supplies experiments. Walk a disputed route. Stand at a witness’s window. Wait through an office to see who crosses. Check a locked threshold from both sides. Compare chant clarity with a claimed listening position. These actions should produce local observations only when physically performed. Study jumps, QA teleports and loads must never fabricate traversals or retroactively observed events.

Environmental guidance can be a page-bearing person, a lamp, a visible repair bundle, chant from an opening or a shelfmark corresponding to a place. None needs an exclamation mark. Text must remain readable and translated; difficulty should come from interpretation, not tiny fonts, undocumented Latin or hidden one-pixel hotspots. Provide scalable text, transcripts for significant audio clues, adjustable look sensitivity and an optional notebook reminder of the question rather than solution arrows.

## Time, people and access

Canonical hours are shared temporal landmarks and different social opportunities. The player learns a day through bells, chant, work stopping, meals, lamps and emptied rooms. Current fixed clock windows are a reconstruction/gameplay convention, not a claim that every medieval community used modern wall-clock times.

For the slice, one advancing day and three physically continuous actor routes suffice. Each actor has an ID, room/route position, duty, possession and current availability. A phase change sends an actor along a route; it does not teleport the same body or silently swap an identity. Streaming a presentation must not delete the simulated actor. The current browser proximity pool and native crowd benchmark are not that simulation.

Access follows duty, permission, the player’s demonstrated purpose and safe egress. A denied entrance may make a claim testable, not just create a key hunt. Necessary evidence must remain obtainable after a missed window: the next routine, an alternate record or an explicit later appointment. Allow diegetic waiting at a bench and pausing for reading/notebook use. Waiting advances scheduled movement and events coherently; there is no hidden countdown while menus are open. The first case has no irreversible timed failure.

NPC schedules should create constraints and opportunities before becoming a full autonomous social simulator. Three credible people with understandable duties are more valuable than 46 decorative ones. Audio should locate real activity; the sound world must not announce the correct hypothesis. If a deduction depends on hearing, provide a perceptually equivalent accessibility path without an omniscient event log.

## Secrets and optional knowledge

| Layer | Examples | Persistence and truth policy |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed architectural discovery | A blocked former opening, sight line, maintenance approach | Known world property with evidence/reconstruction status; no universal “secret switch” |
| Historical/material discovery | Reused parchment, repaired clasp, work mark, accounting convention | Explain with a sourced reference; distinguish general practice from an abbey-specific inference |
| Personal/social discovery | A promise, correspondence, unacknowledged skill or gift | Original character/scenario truth; revealing it need not solve the main case |
| Institutional discovery | Catalogue omission, patronage, burial dispute, stored surplus | Can recur as context across cases, with an explicit continuity model |
| Symbolic/textual connection | Marginal argument, place-name reading, contrasting interpretations | May remain interpretive; record a reading without certifying it as fact |
| Hour-specific observation | A lamp visible only after work, a work sound heard during an office | Save what was observed when; schedules are coherent and discoverable |
| Unresolved fragment | An ambiguous inscription or lost recipient | No fake completion badge; the world can outlast the case |

Fixed novel secrets have their own spoiler level. Original cases should find interest in ordinary spaces before reusing every concealed route. The small prototype must prove a corridor and reading stand can carry a mystery; the full labyrinth is not a prerequisite for meaningful play.

## Replayability without arbitrary randomness

The principal return invitation is another authored question in a place the player now understands. New documents, access roles, visitors, weather or an alternate starting point change what can be observed. The world’s stable geometry/rhythm make that variation intelligible. Optional personal discoveries and defensible alternative readings add depth without pretending that the same fixed culprit is unknown on replay.

Later, allow authored variants only within a proven causal graph: one of two plausible custodians, a bounded time window, an item placed along one validated route, a testimony wording appropriate to what its speaker could see. Validate reachability, route timing, access, clue availability and unique/accepted explanations for **every** supported variant. Record a variant seed/version in saves. Do not permute person/object/location independently or randomly change witness truth to manufacture novelty. Generator research follows at least two successfully playtested fixed cases.

## Content taxonomy: preserve the layers of truth

Classify individual properties/assertions, not entire buildings or characters. A novel character can have an explicit role, an inferred height, historically reconstructed boots and an original game event.

| Internal class | Meaning | Required provenance |
|---|---|---|
| `NOVEL_CANON` | An explicit extracted assertion | Original claim ID(s), corpus hash, citation, `EXPLICIT` certainty; verify the field actually matches the assertion |
| `TEXTUAL_INFERENCE` | Strong/weak/ambiguous reconstruction from the novel | Claim IDs, original certainty and rationale; retain alternatives |
| `HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION` | Plausible missing institution/material detail based on external history | Source URL/bibliographic ID, relevant date/region, rationale and uncertainty |
| `ORIGINAL_GAME_FICTION` | Authored people/events/documents/case explanations | Scenario/world-authoring ID, authoring version, spoiler scope; no borrowed claim citation claiming the event is canon |
| `SYSTEMIC_VARIATION` | Variable run state chosen by a validated rule | Rule/version/seed, allowed range, provenance of underlying fixed properties |

Literary IDs remain `claim_*`; historical references use `history:*`; case IDs are namespaced. Legacy `BOOK`, `RECON`, `AMBIENT` labels in provenance rows are not mechanically promoted to these classes. `BOOK` still needs claim/field verification, `RECON` can mean textual or historical invention, and unsourced ambient plausibility stays an authored design addition until supported. Existing extraction certainty is never overwritten. The extraction pipeline continues to use only the novel.

Internally record `{class, certainty, references, rationale, scenario_id, spoiler_level}` with an assertion/value. Runtime public packages include compact provenance and approved paraphrases, not raw research fragments. The player can open an optional “basis” panel on inspection: textual evidence, reconstruction decision or game fiction. No giant label over every prop is necessary. Starting a scenario clearly says whether it is original or novel-derived.

Keep **content provenance** separate from **in-world reliability**: an original fictional witness can make a true or false claim in that fiction; a historical practice can be used in a fictional incident. One label cannot express both. No original case may add invented events to `book_details/output/`.

## Next playable milestone: The Leaf Before Vespers

**One 25–40-minute original investigation in the already migrated public cloister/church footprint.** Three original people, three short readable artifacts, one recoverable leaf, six-to-eight useful observations, one hour transition and one temporary access rule. Reuse existing normalized monk/scribe/novice presentations with explicit original IDs. No Alinardo hint, skull mechanism or ossuary route is needed.

### Draft authored truth and the player’s question

A loose final leaf of a gathering is missing from a loaned manuscript prepared for return. A novice was seen carrying a wrapped book and is blamed. The copyist moved the leaf to a choir volume to press a damp edge flat; he concealed the damage by giving an imprecise account of when/where he left it. The novice’s wrapped item was a different repair parcel. Recover the correct leaf and establish what the sighting can actually prove. Whether the copyist’s omission deserves punishment can remain a judgement, separate from the established account.

This is a draft truth to be checked against actual anchors/visibility and a historically chosen manuscript exemplar. It does not claim a historical preservation practice merely because it is plausible. If pressing a damp edge in another book lacks an appropriate source, identify it as this character’s improvised mistake, not standard monastic conservation. Before implementation, freeze an internally consistent event table, item-custody chain and route timing; never change the answer ad hoc to fit a convenient scene.

### Reasoning paths

1. **Identity:** compare the physical leaf/edge/stub and its text against the loan description. A catchword at the gathering boundary links to the next gathering’s opening; the novice’s parcel has a different size/wrapping/content. Picking up any page does not solve this.
2. **Custody:** the loan slip and copyist’s changing explanation separate original custody, temporary relocation and return. Finding the leaf in the choir identifies location, not automatically its mover.
3. **Time/place:** a claimed sighting must fit a measured route, a real sight line and the office transition. The novice’s actual movement and an alternate contemporaneous record can challenge the witness if the player missed the live crossing. Final anchor positions and interval lengths come from the existing scene, not invented metre values in this document.

Each path needs an observation the player can interpret, not an interaction toast that states its conclusion. The case has two plausible early accounts (theft/withholding versus mistaken custody), a physical test that discriminates them and a final supported account. Optional damage/motive evidence adds nuance. A critical clue cannot depend solely on seeing a seconds-long animation once.

### Required small systems

Extend the existing domain with attributed claims/observations, a tiny authored event timeline and conclusion links. Add three persistent actors with fixed routes in the loaded public footprint; no whole-abbey AI/navmesh project. Add a readable comparison surface and one case-aware save version that preserves clock, custody, actor progress, access, observations and the provisional account. Record significant sound events only when perceptible; offer a transcript/alternate visual cue.

Use a bell/office transition and available recorded ambience for guidance. One restricted chapel/reading-space threshold can close during an office while permitting exit; the relevant geometry and rule need validation before placement. Prefer a normal door/permission or attention constraint, not an invented secret lock. If that threshold requires new large geometry, reduce the case footprint or use a supervised reading stand instead.

### Entry gates

Run real Windows W1/W2 and Mac human input/audio review on a hash-identified package. Resolve platform-critical failures; record Vulkan/D3D12 separately. Profile the stair unload hitch and make disposal bounded if the diagnosed release path is responsible. Check the multi-shot capture anomaly before using automatic visual comparisons as acceptance evidence. Preserve the old altar/save/pose/audio gates and literature hashes. If hardware is unavailable, do useful hardening/specification and leave Windows explicitly open; no engine commitment or broad migration follows from a headless substitute.

### Exit gates: “Is there a game here?”

- An unfamiliar player can enter, understand the question, explore and submit a supported account without developer teleportation or live coaching.
- Three actors retain identity, travel continuously, become available/unavailable for understandable reasons and survive save/reload mid-route.
- Observation and testimony remain distinguishable; at least one initially plausible account is rejected by a physical/documentary/time comparison the player performs.
- There are two orders of obtaining essential evidence and an alternate path for a missed time window. Waiting, leaving/revisiting and loading do not fabricate observations or soft-lock the case.
- A conclusion needs identity, custody and temporal/visibility support; finding the leaf alone cannot auto-complete it. Incorrect/partial accounts return targeted non-spoiling feedback.
- Save/relaunch preserves the investigation and world state, including an unfinished hypothesis. Old proof saves either migrate explicitly or are rejected clearly without overwrite.
- The existing proof’s performance gates still hold. New three-actor routes and hour/access transitions have their own frame/memory captures. Human listening/input checks are recorded separately from objective tests.
- Playtest with at least three people unfamiliar with the code. Collect completion path/time, wrong inferences, hint dependence, moments of uncertainty and whether they would play a second case. Do not report developer completion as player validation.

The mission stops after this case and its evidence. Excluded: full Aedificium/library migration, 46-person simulation, procedural cases, combat, voiced dialogue, new character pipeline, broad world/art pass and the new web viewer. If the test is dull, revise clue/reasoning design before importing another building. Success licenses a second authored case and only the world sections it requires.
