# Game direction and decisions

**A first-person historical investigation game: arrive as Adapted Adso beside William, learn a working abbey through residence, and use personally acquired knowledge to form increasingly independent interpretations.** A continuous multi-day campaign contains bounded authored investigations. The campaign is the product structure; authored cases are a content-production unit. These are current product decisions, not claims that campaign gameplay is implemented.

The place should feel cold, austere, inhabited, intellectually charged and often quiet. Ordinary work, prayer, meals, weather and recurring people establish normality before deviations become evidence. Architecture supplies tests: walk a disputed route, compare a view from a window, remember which stair joins which floor, or ask whether a sound could have reached a witness. Someone should care about the result through duty, trust, punishment, access, memory or work.

The active mission and its acceptance gates live in [Project](PROJECT.md#active-mission--day-1a). Day-1A asks whether ordinary life is compelling before a mystery starts. The last committed native proof established engine feasibility, not that experience.

## Adapted Adso and William

Protected major source facts remain protected. The player owns attention, exploration, local investigation order, optional relationships, interpretation, confidence, the working notebook and original local outcomes where canon does not determine them. This is an interactive adaptation, not a minute-by-minute reenactment or a parallel story in which the important action always happens elsewhere.

| Progression | What the player learns |
|---|---|
| PLACE | Unknown abbey → familiar routes → spatial reasoning |
| TIME | Bells as atmosphere → offices and routines → predictions → meaningful deviations |
| PEOPLE | Anonymous figures → names/roles → habits/limitations → relationships and unusual behavior |
| METHOD | Assumption → observation/testimony/document → tested inference and honest uncertainty |
| INDEPENDENCE | William leads → asks → Adso chooses tests → residents approach Adso → William relies on Adso's local observations |

These are learning dimensions, not visible stats or skill unlocks. The player becoming capable and Adso developing should substantially overlap.

William teaches method by changing the question, not supplying a room or certifying every conclusion. He is warm, fallible, hungry, cold, tired, humorous and capable of being locally wrong. His broader intellectual understanding can coexist with Adso's better knowledge of lived routines. Early William walks ahead; later they walk together; eventually William may follow Adso. In the library Adso first holds light for William, then directs it, then leads.

No older-Adso narrator during normal play is the current tendency. Sparse eventual framing remains unresolved. Retain the Round-3 dissent: if players wait for William, feel constrained by what Adso would do, or experience William's absence as the loss of the real protagonist, revisit the relationship and protagonist choice from playtest evidence. An original companion is a contingency, not the current plan.

Physical verbs should stay sparse and contextual: walk, look, carry, place, hold, open, close, sit, wait, show, ask, write and inspect. Do not make every prop or shelf interactive. ## Library: The Remembered Labyrinth

**Signature secondary pillar. Core loop: ORIENT → COMPARE → TEST → RECORD.** The fixed library starts uncertain, then becomes recognizable through rooms, routes, inscriptions, books and classification. The physical world does not shift; Adso's incomplete mental model can be wrong. A substantial visit should usually start with a question from the living abbey and end with a consequence outside it.

The preferred mapping direction is a diegetic hybrid sketch: visited rooms and walked connections can be remembered, while uncertain relations stay uncertain. No perfect global floor plan, GPS dot or unexplored-room reveal by default. A rigid no-map rule is also unaccepted; exact UI and accessibility remain open. Bells, wind, windows and lamps can support orientation without encoding a correct turn.

Approximately four or five meaningful entries is a working cadence, not a locked schedule. Day 1 keeps the library inaccessible and present through architecture/rules. The later native prototype is 10–12 rooms with about seven in its cognitive core: uncertainty, recognition, temporary loss, recovery, one knowledge-assisted search and a repeat visit. Test this before migrating all 56 browser rooms. No major protected literary secret is needed for the first test.

The full [Day-1 design](../story-council/day-1/OPUS_DAY1_DESIGN.md), authored by Claude Opus 5.5, asks: **what is this house, and how does it behave?** Its estimated 85–90 minute full day and 60–75 minute prototype have not been playtested. The owner's 2026-10-05 handoff replaces its broad minimum with two missions:

| Stage | Content | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Day-1A, active | Arrival, William's lead/wait relationship, gate, repeated routes, guest room, ordinary meal, a few residents, free period, Nones | Prove that ordinary abbey life is worth inhabiting; exact brief in PROJECT |
| Day-1B, planned | Snow in the Straw, small seen/told/inference notebook, Vespers, night ending and Day-2 hook | Begin only after Day-1A passes |

**Snow in the Straw is ORIGINAL_GAME_FICTION.** A guest-house worker leaves a shutter on a hook; changing wind and a failed old frame/staple let it blow open and admit snow. The guest keeper blames the worker. Physical damage, wind, testimony, timing and Adso's own remembered placement of things can support different qualities of conclusion. Design names Nuto/Tebaldo/Fulco remain provisional until canon/name review. William asks, “When did it open?” and leaves Adso to test the question. Consequences are modest and human, with no failure screen.

The accepted full-day ending is William asking, “What did you see today that I could not?” and, for tomorrow, “Bring me one thing tomorrow night that I could not have seen.” Those original illustrative lines express growing independence; the scene belongs to Day-1B.

Keep arrival-road/reception/audience, weather, supper, west portal, original-name/office collisions and William's tone on the finished-book canon-review list. Do not infer clearance from architectural extraction or supply forward spoilers to the owner. Dialogue in the design is intent; its estimated 280–330 lines must not become an automatic implementation target.

## Reasoning and later scenario systems

Day-1A needs only the bounded foundation. Day-1B should introduce simple statements, people memory, **seen versus told**, and one modest “I think…” inference. A full comparison board, free-text notebook, general dialogue RPG or all-purpose scenario language is not authorized by that first step.

Record “Brother Martin says he left the leaf before Vespers,” not “leaf left before Vespers.” An inspection preserves observable features rather than the explanation. Finding an object establishes location, not authorship or motive. Notes retain speaker/document, place, time, modality and uncertainty. Earlier mistaken notes can remain and be annotated rather than silently replaced by truth. Some questions can remain unresolved.

Later investigations use authored causal truth and supported alternatives. The player observes, forms a question, follows a plausible lead, compares, tests, records and revisits under changed conditions. Ensure alternate evidence paths for critical conclusions and missed time windows. QA jumps, loads and waiting cannot fabricate things Adso supposedly saw. A useful conclusion receives context, access or human consequences; no XP or collectible completion percentage is needed.

Canonical hours are social landmarks: work stops, people move, meals happen, church use changes and doors close with safe egress. Current numerical hour windows are a reconstruction convention. Persistent actors need stable identities and continuous physical routes; a streamed presentation is not the actor's whole state. A few credible recurring people are the priority. Pausing/reading and intentional waiting must not hide irreversible timers or soft-lock a scenario.

Sound stays physically embedded: silence dominates, chant is people singing somewhere, bells are independent events, work sounds correspond to work, surfaces affect footsteps and openings/doors matter. Use continuous attenuation/filtering rather than abrupt zones. Supplied tracks remain unassigned until listened to; titles and waveform measurements do not establish suitability. Significant audio evidence needs an equivalent accessible path, not an omniscient event log.

## A reusable scenario is an event/evidence model

A scenario overlays a stable world; it is not a list of “go here, press E” quests. The minimum design contract is:

| Primitive | Required information |
|---|---|
| Identity/header | Scenario/version, required world/data version, continuity/date, role, spoiler level, entry knowledge, completion policy |
| Starting condition | What actually happened before entry, what is still happening and what the player is told |
| People | Stable scenario IDs, templates, duties, knowledge, custody/permissions, authored statements; presentation pool never invents identity |
| Locations/routes | Existing room/portal/anchor IDs, navigable routes, travel lower bounds, visibility/acoustic paths and access windows |
| Objects/documents | Stable IDs, authored readable content, physical state/custody, provenance and inspection affordances |
| Events | Preconditions, start/end time or causal trigger, participants, physical actions, resulting state; authored past events distinguished from observed ones |
| Observations | Perceivable condition, source, place/time/modality, recording rule, what it does and does not establish |
| Claims | Speaker/document, wording, time scope, certainty and referenced event; false claims never become “known facts” automatically |
| Contradictions | Competing accounts and the observation/constraint that challenges them; unavailable evidence is not evidence of absence |
| Hypotheses | Authored candidate explanations, required links, legitimate alternatives, disconfirming evidence and unanswered questions |
| Outcomes | An explanation’s acceptance conditions and justified consequences; object recovery separate from authorship/motive/accusation |
| Persistence | Scenario/version, time, events/custody, actor route progress, access, observations, claims, player links, submitted account and unresolved state |

These are a planned contract for later campaign/investigation work; the full model is outside Day-1A. Existing shared schemas do not implement all these primitives. Start with one data-driven case plus a few typed domain services, not an all-purpose narrative language. Reuse the closed condition/action vocabulary and add a primitive only when the slice needs it. No arbitrary code expressions in scenario data.

A future layout might hold base world records in `shared/data`, authored overlays in `shared/scenarios/<case>/`, and native adapters that bind them to scenes. Do not create empty scenario folders now or duplicate base rooms inside each case. Saves require scenario ID/version and a migration strategy before packs ship; case changes cannot reinterpret an old saved accusation silently.

## Content taxonomy: preserve the layers of truth

Classify individual properties/assertions, not entire buildings or characters. A novel character can have an explicit role, an inferred height, historically reconstructed boots and an original game event.

| Internal class | Meaning | Required provenance |
|---|---|---|
| `NOVEL_CANON` | An explicit extracted assertion | Original claim ID(s), corpus hash, citation, `EXPLICIT` certainty; verify the field actually matches the assertion |
| `TEXTUAL_INFERENCE` | Strong/weak/ambiguous reconstruction from the novel | Claim IDs, original certainty and rationale; retain alternatives |
| `HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION` | Plausible missing institution/material detail based on external history | Source URL/bibliographic ID, relevant date/region, rationale and uncertainty |
| `ORIGINAL_GAME_FICTION` | Authored people/events/documents/case explanations | Scenario/world-authoring ID, authoring version, spoiler scope; no borrowed claim citation claiming the event is canon |
| `SYSTEMIC_VARIATION` | Variable run state chosen by a validated rule | Rule/version/seed, allowed range, provenance of underlying fixed properties |

Literary IDs remain `claim_*`; historical references use `history:*`; case IDs are namespaced. Legacy `BOOK`, `RECON`, `AMBIENT` labels in provenance rows are not mechanically promoted to these classes. `BOOK` still needs claim/field verification, `RECON` can mean textual or historical invention, and unsourced ambient plausibility stays an authored design addition until supported. Existing extraction certainty is never overwritten. The extraction pipeline continues to use only the novel.

Internally record `{class, certainty, references, rationale, scenario_id, spoiler_level}` with an assertion/value. Runtime public packages include compact provenance and approved paraphrases, not raw research fragments. The player can open an optional “basis” panel on inspection: textual evidence, reconstruction decision or game fiction. No giant label over every prop is necessary. Starting a scenario clearly says whether it is original or novel-derived.

Keep **content provenance** separate from **in-world reliability**: an original fictional witness can make a true or false claim in that fiction; a historical practice can be used in a fictional incident. One label cannot express both. No original case may add invented events to `book_details/output/`.
