## Initial realism brief

You are continuing the interactive Three.js reconstruction of Umberto Eco's *The Name of the Rose* in `.`. The application is structurally implemented and playable. Your assignment is a substantial visual, environmental, character, animal, sound and immersion pass: move the player experience from visibly procedural reconstruction to a credible, atmospheric, historically coherent abbey in late November 1327.

### Import, rights and performance requirements

Check free disk space before downloading large source WAVs and making web derivatives. This volume had about 211 MiB free during the follow-up audio audit; preserve the book extraction and project files.

**Use the internet and existing external assets when they clearly beat procedural reconstruction.** Download, adapt, retexture and optimize suitable models, PBR maps, animations and sounds instead of sculpting every horse, monk, chair and tree from primitive code. Preview any proposed model alongside the current abbey before committing. Use asset authors, licenses, source URLs, conversion steps and local derivative paths in a checked-in provenance manifest and expose CC BY credits to players. CC0 and CC BY can be shipped as licensed; paid Fab/CGTrader assets need the purchased tier and **explicit suitability for public web delivery** checked first. Do not use CC BY-NC/ND, contradictory “personal use only” demos or ripped game assets. Do not purchase an asset merely because it is listed here; free alternatives and proof-of-concept imports are useful first.

Use GLB/glTF web derivatives and appropriate loader/animation support. Normalize units and axes; keep ground contacts, material color space and rigs correct. Put simplified colliders around imported props only where the walker should be blocked; do not let decorative meshes seal doors or corridors. NPC and animal bodies should avoid furniture, wall and each other; feet/hooves should meet the floor/snow and mangers should meet muzzles. Set LOD, near/distant texture resolution, KTX2, geometry compression, shared materials/skeleton strategy, culling, animation update distance and shadow budgets appropriate to a browser. The existing merged architecture and distance gating are performance advantages. Measure download size, draw calls, memory and frame time at the most crowded hour and in active snow; trim source assets into web-ready versions rather than loading high-poly originals. [Three.js GLTFLoader documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html) covers Draco/Meshopt/KTX2 support.

### Definition of done

The abbey remains playable and recognizably follows the extracted novel constraints. From normal walking height, the stable, church, scriptorium, kitchen, refectory, infirmary, baths, smithy, cemetery and crypt communicate their function before a label appears. Humans and livestock look anatomical and belong to the environment; focal props have convincing form, material and contact. No ossuary spike fills the camera; no grave grid, cartoon skull panel, featureless bed/tub or repeated primitive animal survives in a featured view. Darkness is atmospheric but navigable, and snow/mud/stone/wood behavior is coherent. **With sound enabled, footsteps, animal calls, bells, chant, fire, wind and work actions sound like real recorded sources; none of the prominent synthetic voices or impacts remains.** The mix is spatial and place-specific, without modern noise or actionless close cues. New assets load reliably and the scene remains responsive.

When finished, report what was replaced versus polished, exact shipped model/texture/**audio** provenance and licenses, source clip crop times and any conditional candidates declined, performance measurements, how you verified all canonical hours/weather and major walkable rooms **with sound on**, and any remaining visual or audible limitations.
