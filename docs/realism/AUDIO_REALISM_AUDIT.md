# Audio realism audit and recorded-source shortlist — 29 September 2026

## Scope and evidence

The user reports that the current sounds are terrible. The source explains why: `src/systems/audio.js`, `src/systems/audio/{banks,steps,chant,emitters,dsp,reverb}.js` generate every audible source from noise, oscillators, filters and envelopes. There are no recorded audio files in the app. I traced the runtime calls and the fixed source placement in `src/world/soundscape.js`, then enabled the Sound checkbox in the running stable scene; the browser showed sound enabled and logged no audio error. The browser/tool environment did not provide an audio-input path for subjective listening, so claims about exact perceived timbre are **code-based diagnoses plus the user's direct listening report**, not an assertion that I heard each cue. The candidate pages were checked for descriptions and displayed licenses; their downloaded audio still requires audition and cleanup by Agent A.

The current **spatial architecture is worth keeping**: surface classification, foot timing, positional panners, distance limits, zone occlusion, canonical-hour scheduling, bell position, voice cap and two-slot convolver transitions. A source replacement should use those systems. The procedural source banks and live `Chant` should cease to be the audible foreground. Quiet synthesized air can remain only if it survives a blind comparison with a recorded bed.

## Chant rights and historical fit

The music is the hardest source to solve properly. [Neumz](https://neumz.com/) documents real Benedictine office recordings and is a useful **performance/liturgical reference**, but free app playback is not a reuse license. [Wikimedia Commons' Johannes hymn](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Johannes.Hymnus.ogg) is a real human performance released to the **public domain**, but it is a solo hymn, not a suitable ready-made Matins/choir loop. A more promising **conditional real ensemble recording** is [Schola Gregoriana, *Deus in adjutorium meum intende*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Schola_Gregoriana-Deus_in_adjutorium_meum_intende.ogg), a 58-second Latin Vespers opening recorded in 2007 and released **CC BY-SA 3.0** with documented performer authorization. Its source service honors [Vincent Pallotti](https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/vincenzo-pallotti.html), who lived centuries after 1327, so it is not a ready-made service for this abbey; Agent A must verify whether the generic opening excerpt is period/office appropriate, credit the choir and comply with share-alike terms for any edited derivative. The related Magnificat/antiphon for Pallotti should not be presented as an abbey office in 1327. [Gregobase](https://gregobase.selapa.net/?page_id=2) releases chant transcriptions under **CC0**; scores do not grant rights to other people's recordings. The preferred shippable route is a new short recording by several human male voices performing appropriate office material, with performer and recording rights documented. A licensed Latin Gregorian performance may also work if its recording-specific terms explicitly permit this public web app. Make chant a timed office event with rests, responses and a clear end, rather than an endless background pad.

## Integration and acceptance

1. Build a small provenance-backed audio manifest mapping each current bank name to one or more real source files, with author, URL, license snapshot, crop times and conversion notes. Keep sources out of the initial browser download; ship short compressed Ogg/Opus or broadly supported alternative derivatives, loading by proximity/zone. Local disk was nearly full at this audit, so check capacity before downloading large WAV masters.
2. Compare each candidate **in the actual scene at equal loudness**. Reject poor audio even if the license is perfect. Cut at zero crossings, add short fades, remove modern contamination, create several nonidentical footsteps/calls, and keep natural attack/tail. Do not pitch-shift voice or bell into cartoon territory.
3. Keep `Sound` API and zone/horarium logic, but make close human/animal/work/door cues event-driven by visible animation and contact. Fixed emitters may provide *distant* offscreen beds only. Silence during sleeping hours and in secret spaces matters as much as new samples.
4. Walk at least gate avenue, cloister, nave during an office, scriptorium during work, kitchen, stable, folds, smithy, cemetery, library and crypt **with sound enabled**. Check dry source, indoor/outdoor threshold, full spatial mix, headphones and ordinary speakers. No recognizably synthetic hoof, bell, pig, cluck, quill, fire or chant should remain in a featured scene; no modern machine, road, TV or language bleed should enter the abbey.
