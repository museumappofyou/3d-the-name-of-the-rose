# Visual reference board

These linked photo galleries, collection objects and historical reconstructions are **visual references, not drop-in texture/image assets**. Observe construction, use and light; do not copy their plans over Eco's extracted constraints. The abbey in the novel is a fictional 1327 Italian Benedictine setting; English/Cistercian/Dominican examples help fill unspecified detail but differ in order, date and location.

| Domain / reference | What Agent A should study and apply |
|---|---|
| **Italian Romanesque/Cistercian stone and cloister:** [Italian Ministry of Culture, Abbazia di Fossanova gallery](https://cultura.gov.it/luogo/abbazia-di-fossanova), [museum page](https://direzioneregionalemuseilazio.cultura.gov.it/en/luoghi/abbazia-di-fossanova/) | Photographs of church facade, cloister, chapter room and aerial group. Note that stone blocks vary in tone and size, returns and opening reveals have depth, roofs have differentiated planes, and the cloister has measured rhythm. Apply surface and scale lessons to existing geometry; preserve Eco's Aedificium and abbey layout. |
| **Monastic functional precinct:** [English Heritage Rievaulx Abbey description](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/rievaulx-abbey/history-and-stories/history/description/), [Byland description](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/byland-abbey/history/description/) | Ranges support distinct uses: church/choir, dormitory, infirmary, kitchen, refectory, livestock and workyard. Study thresholds, relative volumes and the way service areas gather tools, food, refuse and traffic wear. Historical plans are comparison only. |
| **Church nave, choir and furniture:** [Fossanova church images](https://cultura.gov.it/luogo/abbazia-di-fossanova), [Gloucester Blackfriars](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/blackfriars/history/), [Eco church extraction](../research/book/CHURCH.md) | Separate monks' choir stalls and the more open nave; study thick columns, deeply set windows and stone floors. Eco specifies colored glass, many wooden furnishings and a singular giant bronze tripod lamp at night. Avoid importing later uniform pew geometry as default. |
| **Vault geometry and ceremonial decoration:** [Westminster Abbey Chapter House and Pyx Chamber gallery](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/chapter-house-and-pyx-chamber/history/), [Cleeve Abbey history](https://production.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/cleeve-abbey/history/) | Compare vault surface continuity, rib-to-column joins, stone thickness, floor tile age and selective paint traces. Westminster restoration includes Victorian work, so use geometry/light cues, not every ornament as a 1327 template. |
| **Scriptorium/work positions:** [Blackfriars surviving study carrels](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/blackfriars/history/), [English Heritage Byland inkstand](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/byland-abbey/history/collection/), [Met monasticism essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/monasticism-in-western-medieval-europe) | Desk scale, windows, shelving and the range of writing apparatus: quills, ink, sand, parchment, knives and lecterns. Use book count/layout first (40 windows/desks), then make a few detailed stations and varied work poses. |
| **Monk garments and posture:** [Met Medieval Art educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/medieval.pdf), [Italian monastic manuscript examples](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2003/italian-manuscript-painting) | Tunic, cowl, stockings/shoes, tonsure and the way coarse cloth hangs around shoulders, knees and wrists. Keep population visually restrained, vary hood, age and posture. Later images are form references, not costume patterns to copy literally. |
| **Refectory and chapter:** [Bury St Edmunds Abbey history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/bury-st-edmunds-abbey/history/), [Cleeve refectory tile reference](https://production.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/cleeve-abbey/history/) | Long tables/benches along walls, shared quiet meal, worn tile and an appropriate amount of equipment; avoid identical bowl rows and “modern canteen” spacing. Bury's grave slab photos also show weathered surface difference. |
| **Cemetery/grave forms:** [Monk Bretton grave slab examples](https://production.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/monk-bretton-priory/history/), [Lanercost medieval tombs](https://production.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/lanercost-priory/history/a-family-mausoleum/), [Byland 12th-century slab](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/byland-abbey/history/collection/), [Holycross documented slab scan](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/holycross-grave-slab-tn047-030019-69082e64b9274888b02ad244b0197d11) | Thin worn incised surfaces, imperfect edges, different ages/heights and occasional almost-buried markers; not identical upright crosses. Eco's extracted cemetery description requires old and new stones with only some protruding above snow. The Holycross model is reference or retopology source, not a 863k-triangle repeat asset. |
| **Stable working detail:** [Historic England farm building guidance](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/maintenance-repair-trad-farm-buildings/heag157-maintenance-repair-traditional-farm-buildings/), [traditional stable elements](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/historic-farmsteads-preliminary-character-statement-south-west/historic-farmsteads-south-west-part3/) | Study manger and hayrack height, worn floors, ventilation, bedding, threshold grime and feed storage. These references document many later buildings: avoid copying Victorian ironwork/brick wholesale. The novel's metal grille and mangers control this reconstruction. |
| **Winter roof and terrain behavior:** [Snowy Alpine village roofs, Wikimedia photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Clocher_et_toits_de_Sainte-Foy-Tarentaise_enneig%C3%A9s_%28novembre_2025%29.JPG), [Poly Haven Snow 03](https://polyhaven.com/a/snow_03), [Brown Mud 03](https://polyhaven.com/a/brown_mud_03) | Snow sits unevenly on eaves/ledges; walked routes become dirty/compressed, exposed yard earth is wet, wall feet and roof edges transition gradually. Alpine photo gives accumulation cues, not a location template. Eco's opening snow is shallow. |
| **Crypt/ossuary light:** [Fossanova interiors](https://cultura.gov.it/luogo/abbazia-di-fossanova), [Westminster Pyx Chamber history](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/chapter-house-and-pyx-chamber/history/) | Thick rough columns and restrained practical illumination; darkness should retain space contours and material response. In game, use lantern and single point sources without orange clipping or near-black loss. Use book-specified skull/bone organization for props. |

Reference use order: (1) [book extraction](../research/book) for factual constraints; (2) surviving period or near-period built examples for missing construction details; (3) material scans and photographic lighting for surface behavior; (4) candidate game assets after style, rights, scale and performance checks. Note which visual choices are historical inference rather than literal novel text.
