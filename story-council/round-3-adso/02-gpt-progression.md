**Role:** Player Agency & Progression Systems Designer. **Recommendation:** ADAPTED ADSO.

This is an independent design judgment, not a change to the project's approved direction. All proposed incidents, conversations and supporting residents below are original game fiction. ## 1. The test: does lived knowledge change play?

Adso's development can carry progression, provided the game distinguishes **knowing more**, **reasoning better**, and **being permitted to do more**. These are different changes. Visiting twelve rooms does not make an inference sound. A resident accepting a supported explanation does not grant universal authority. A familiar routine does not establish what happened on a particular evening.

Progression is real when an earlier experience changes a later decision. A player remembers an ordinary entrance, anticipates when a worker will be available, chooses a discriminating question, or notices that a familiar arrangement has changed. They finish a later problem with fewer unnecessary journeys and a better account because they understand the institution. The game never needs to announce an improvement.

It is false progression if the same question becomes solvable only after collecting enough observations; if dialogue marked “perceptive” unlocks after three visits; or if a notebook silently supplies the inference Adso has supposedly learned to make. Hidden experience points would undermine the hypothesis as thoroughly as visible ones.

The earlier councils correctly separated human memory, character records and persistent world consequences. I retain that distinction, but reject their occasional suggestion that an essential experience should be usable only if the player deliberately wrote it down. Manual clerical diligence should not determine eligibility to reason. Preserve encountered source material lightly; let the player choose interpretations and emphasis.

None of this requires Adso. Models C and D could run the same mechanics. Adso earns his place if these decisions feel like the growth of a recognizable young companion rather than the improvement of a generic investigator. His relationship with William makes the changing standard of evidence personal; his arrival makes player ignorance and character ignorance align. That is a strong fit, although prototypes must establish that the name adds value beyond recognition.

## 2. Four identities, considered seriously

### A — Strict canonical Adso

The strongest version is a focused literary investigation, not an imitation of an open role-playing game. Canonical facts can remain fixed while players independently establish them. A known conclusion does not prevent a meaningful experiment, just as solving an authored mystery does not require its truth to be random.

The difficulty is what happens when the player reaches a conclusion early, misses an experience the characterization assumes, or prioritizes a different conversation. Strict chronology and closely prescribed dialogue leave little room to acknowledge these differences. Original matters need particularly careful placement and may distort the apparent importance of ordinary moments. Readers may value exact recognition, but every extra scene increases continuity and spoiler review.

Emotional identity is exceptionally clear. Replay can explore evidence order and readings, although it cannot promise substantially different events. Meaningful player conclusions remain possible; meaningful ownership of the whole stay is harder. This model suits a shorter, tightly authored adaptation. It is a weaker fit for this project's persistent familiarity and original-matter ambitions.

### B — Adapted Adso

Adso remains explicitly Adso. The game openly compresses, adds and reorganizes experiences as an interactive adaptation. It preserves the relationship and recognizable foundations while giving the player ownership of attention, tests, local commitments and supported accounts.

This preserves the value of the identity because adaptation freedom concerns how he learns, not whether he becomes an unrelated personality. Respect, curiosity, uncertainty and disagreement can coexist. The player need not choose a contemporary worldview or perform obedience to every sentence William says.

The danger is an adaptation label being used to excuse any contradiction. Protected source facts still require review. But a declared original reading-place dispute does not have to impersonate a missing chapter. B gives enough agency if local outcomes actually change and William responds to player reasoning. A fixed central history with a few interchangeable dialogue tones would not be sufficient.

### C — Original companion travelling with William

This is the best fallback. It retains arrival, apprenticeship and growing familiarity while allowing original dialogue, relationships, errors and future scenarios. A purpose independent of William would prevent the companion becoming his travelling quest interface.

The cost is concrete: the game must explain why this person occupies the companion's position, whether this is a separate continuity, and why William's attention matters to them. A new protagonist does not automatically escape William's narrative dominance. All the same mentorship safeguards are necessary.

C offers broader outcomes and expansion, but makes the literary connection less immediate. If the central promise is solving many original institutional problems, C may be preferable. If it is becoming capable beside William, B communicates that promise with less narrative setup.

### D — Original abbey/local assistant

D has the strongest built-in divided loyalty. Duties and relationships belong to the player before William arrives. Local evidence can challenge a visitor's interpretation from the beginning, and withholding information has an independent social basis.

Its problem is the learning curve. An established resident should know routes and customs the human does not. Artificial amnesia, tutorial ignorance or constant internal exposition would damage the role. A recent appointment could mitigate this, but would weaken the local advantage.

D is a stronger game about institutional responsibility and competing obligations. It is a weaker game about discovering a place alongside a mentor. It should be selected deliberately for that different fantasy, not merely to solve Adso's constraints.

## 3. Agency: understanding must have consequences

There are three layers: what happened, what Adso can establish, and what he does with that account. Keep the first stable within an investigation. Allow substantial variation in the second and third.

The player may investigate in different orders, visit different residents, select a physical or documentary test, choose which small problems to help with, explore permitted ordinary spaces, and record competing interpretations. They may give William a cautious report rather than an accusation, ask a witness privately rather than publicly, or delay sharing a harmless uncertainty while testing it. Optional relationships can concern attention, assistance and discretion without adding unsupported character histories.

Those choices should produce different local outcomes. A corrected misunderstanding can spare an original resident embarrassment; an overconfident report can make that resident less willing to volunteer personal information; a discreet clarification can preserve a promise. Choosing a reading place can change who shares it and which ordinary conversation happens there. These consequences must appear in later behavior, not only in an ending paragraph.

Consider an original disputed delivery. Two people repeat an account, but one learned it from the other. Adso may ask each independently, reconstruct a route, or inspect an ordinary receipt. The delivery's past remains fixed. He can establish movement while leaving authorship uncertain, clear a particular accusation without proving every detail, or prematurely blame a worker. The community's response varies within authored bounds. More evidence should improve what he can justify, not force a prescribed emotional interpretation.

The player cannot legitimately choose wholesale reinvention of Adso's identity, arbitrary violence, unsupported betrayals, or actions offered as capable of overturning protected source outcomes. Do not offer “prevent this” if prevention has already been ruled out by the adaptation contract. Show a fixed development as a development; place playable decisions where they can matter.

Temporary withholding needs limits. “I want to verify this before naming someone” is a defensible choice. Concealing a known urgent threat merely to create a dramatic branch is a much larger characterization decision. Protect such material rather than treating every withheld statement as equally available. The player may disagree with William about evidence without turning disagreement into permanent hostility.

Facts remaining fixed while interpretation differs is necessary, but insufficient. A purely private opinion would make agency decorative. The player's understanding should change their next question, their chosen test, whom they involve, and what local action follows. Major history can remain protected while these changes are substantial.

Conclusions should distinguish support from conviction. “I am certain” is an assertion for which Adso accepts responsibility, not a resource that improves success chance. “I cannot establish who carried it” can be the strongest available account. An evaluator may identify an unsupported step, but should not identify the next clue or insist that every uncertainty be removed.

## 4. Progression without levels

The central loop is: live an ordinary practice, form an expectation, encounter a discrepancy, select a test, revise an account, and see what that account changes. Not every ordinary experience becomes a later clue. Some establish belonging, pleasure or frustration only.

| Dimension | Initial position | Later capability | Visible evidence of growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial familiarity | Landmarks are isolated impressions | Relate routes, entrances, levels and vantages | Choose a plausible route; reject a sightline that cannot show an entrance |
| Temporal familiarity | A bell interrupts activity | Anticipate a transition and its social effects | Arrange a conversation before work stops without chasing an icon |
| Social familiarity | Dress or rank suggests identity | Separate duties, habits, expertise and relationships | Ask the worker who handles a task, rather than the highest-status speaker |
| Vocabulary and context | A term carries several unfamiliar meanings | Understand the relevant local use and ask about scope | Distinguish an ordinary rule from a statement about this occasion |
| Observation discipline | A brief impression becomes a whole story | Describe what was actually perceptible | Separate seeing someone leave from knowing their destination |
| Source criticism | Two accounts seem like two confirmations | Trace who learned what from whom | Recognize repeated rumor or a copied entry as dependent evidence |
| Confidence | Seek certainty or William's approval | Commit to supported claims and name limits | Act on a narrow finding while leaving motive unsettled |
| Independence | Borrow William's question | Form a question and choose its test | Bring a discovered problem, not just a requested answer |
| Access | Need an introduction or supervision | Negotiate a specific purpose through a known relationship | A resident lends a working space or agrees to a later appointment |

All reasoning verbs are available from the beginning. An experienced player can classify evidence correctly and initiate a test immediately. The game must acknowledge that competence, not make them wait until Adso's scripted education catches up. Growth comes from richer contexts and fewer supplied questions, with scaffolding reduced in response to demonstrated understanding.

Access is specific and revocable for intelligible reasons. A resident recognizing Adso's care with a document may let him compare two ordinary records under supervision. It does not grant a universal scholar rank. Players who did not earn that relationship retain a viable appointment or alternate evidence path.

### What the player should feel

| Point | Feeling | Gameplay proof |
|---|---|---|
| Hour 1 | Curious, disoriented, already able to choose | A small problem permits two sensible approaches; William does not dictate either |
| Hour 3 | Familiar enough to anticipate | Return to a known person at a useful hour and qualify an earlier assumption |
| Midpoint | Useful on their own terms | Notice a discrepancy without a quest announcement; a resident asks Adso directly |
| Late campaign | Capable, but wary of outdated knowledge | Recognize a changed routine and establish whether an old expectation still applies |
| Ending of playable arc | Responsible for an account they own | Explain a finding and its limits without needing William to supply the conclusion |

The ending feeling does not require total mastery, a prescribed victory or a specific source outcome. Becoming better at uncertainty is part of development.

### Scenario topology must grow too

Begin with a small network: one question, two possible explanations, two ways to test a critical point, and a concrete local consequence. The player may do the test before William asks about it.

Next use a branching investigation whose spatial, social and document threads can be visited in either order. Each thread establishes a different part of the account. Finding the object does not settle custody; establishing custody does not settle intention. Different routes to understanding create different conversations, not different pasts.

At the midpoint, overlap two matters through one ordinary practice or recurring person. Attention becomes a decision: help someone now or make an appointment after the next office. Avoid a mandatory optimal itinerary or irreversible failure for choosing the interesting conversation.

Later, change permissions and uses of known spaces. Keep geometry stable. An ordinary room serving a different purpose changes possible routes and witnesses. Familiarity supplies a hypothesis, not immunity to change. The player must verify the new arrangement.

Every critical finding needs independent support routes. Two documents copied from the same source are not independent. A present-day route experiment establishes current possibility; it does not prove a past door state. A missed observation can be replaced by appropriate contemporary evidence, never by automatically relabelling a later reenactment as something witnessed earlier.

### Hours and memory must remain fair

Bells, changes in work, light and movement communicate time. Do not require the player to learn modern clock values or attend every office. Translate necessary terms in context; uncertainty should come from evidence, not inaccessible vocabulary.

Allow bounded waiting with a stated destination in time. It advances the world without awarding observations from places Adso did not watch. Pause timed simulation during substantial reading and reasoning. A hearing-accessibility alternative must preserve what was perceptible from that position, rather than expose a global event log.

Habit notes need qualifiers: “twice I saw him take this walk,” not “he always takes it.” Absence means absence from the visible area during the watched interval. Provide source-based reminders and a returning-player recap of encountered material. The human's memory may produce fluent immediate questioning; consulting a record should remain an equally legitimate form of competence.

## 5. The notebook as a record of becoming careful

The notebook should evolve in wording and use, not by withholding basic buttons. From the beginning, mechanically preserve the source, encounter, approximate time and place of important information. The player may still write a sloppy inference. The interface must not silently certify it.

| Class | Example from original fiction | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| OBSERVATION | “I saw the door open before Vespers.” | The visible state at that encounter |
| TESTIMONY | “The attendant says it was closed for the office.” | What that person asserts, with its stated scope |
| DOCUMENT | “The work list assigns him to the west walk.” | What the list records, not proof that he obeyed it |
| INFERENCE | “His statement may describe usual practice.” | Adso's proposed explanation |
| UNCERTAINTY | “I cannot establish when it was shut.” | A limit on the present account |

Early prose can merge these ideas: “The door was closed; the attendant told me.” William asks whether Adso saw that state. The player revises it into attributed testimony and adds the observed opening separately. The original line remains, crossed or annotated, with the revision's occasion. If the player was precise initially, William acknowledges it and moves to a harder question about time scope or source dependence.

Later revisions should be player initiated. Categories can be taught through a walking conversation, a disputed note or a comparison of two accounts. They must never become an examination in picking the correct colored tag. Observation is not automatically reliable: distant silhouettes and ambiguous sounds need their own limits.

Provide a brief source page, a few player comparisons and a short provisional account. Free writing is optional; the game should accept bounded structured claims and evidence links without guessing the meaning of prose. Older assumptions remain inspectable as a trace of development, not a mandatory scrapbook to clean up.

Most reasoning happens in the abbey: stand where someone stood, ask what their words cover, watch a transition, compare an object with its description. A note records the result. It should take seconds to consult relevant material, and competent players should close it and act. If the notebook becomes the primary screen, the spatial progression thesis has failed.

## 6. William and Adso: collaboration replaces examination

William's early role is to expose an unsupported step. He might ask, in newly authored dialogue, “Was that what you saw, or what you expected?” He leaves the choice of test open. He should react to the player's actual account, including competent early reasoning, rather than recite a fixed teaching scene.

Walking conversations can follow observations both characters have access to. William contributes an intellectual context; Adso can point out a local detail. They can disagree about whether a rule describes an exception, or whether precision is worth embarrassing someone. Neither participant must win every argument. Humor, tiredness and shared discomfort give these conversations a purpose beyond evidence delivery. A visit to William should sometimes produce a human encounter without a mission.

In the middle, William asks for Adso's question before proposing his own. He can hold an incomplete hypothesis because his information is incomplete. If Adso brings a stronger local account, William changes what he does: takes another route, consults the proposed resident, or retracts a casual assumption. Do not impose a quota of William being wrong once per day. Repetitive corrections would make him an artificial training opponent.

Later, residents become the audience for Adso's work. William neither narrates the discoveries back nor grants permission to conclude. Political activity creates believable periods of separation. When they compare information, Adso supplies local experience and William supplies context unavailable to him. Both contributions retain their sources and limits.

Adso eventually can formulate a question, distinguish its competing explanations, select an experiment, interpret a result and act proportionately without seeking approval. William makes this emotionally visible by listening before framing the problem, citing Adso as the source, changing a plan on his evidence and trusting him to state what remains unknown. These are consequences of reasoning, not affectionate substitutes for agency.

## 7. Older Adso: let the notes carry the reflection

No framing preserves immediacy and avoids implying that every choice is already remembered correctly. It loses a useful reflective texture but is the safest starting position.

Sparse chapter openings and closings can supply atmosphere, yet “I did not understand then” risks announcing a revelation before the player reaches it. Reflection after major investigations can mark changing interpretation, but easily becomes the game's official answer. Both variants require an additional spoiler and knowledge-state review for every line.

The strongest option is **player-shaped notes gradually becoming an account of this adaptation**, without a compulsory older voice. The player revisits an earlier assumption and selects a correction, qualification or unresolved statement. The accumulating account demonstrates development through its own changed language. It never claims to be the novel's actual memoir.

If an older-Adso voice is later retained, confine it to sparse reflections about the act of remembering. It must add no facts, future consequences or privileged interpretations, and never narrate during a live test. Reject it if it makes the young player's account feel like the wrong answer to a memoir already written.

## 8. An original first 90 minutes as Adso

This is a proposed interactive opening, not the novel's first-day chronology. Its recurring residents are an original hospitality assistant and a young attendant. Each has ordinary work beyond serving the player. The small question is: **where can a guest read a borrowed volume without disrupting the next office?**

| Approximate playtime | Experience and decision |
|---|---|
| 0–10 minutes | Arrive with William. Wet wool, colder stone, work sounds and a bell heard from beyond the visible court establish scale. A short walking exchange concerns travel discomfort, not an objective. The hospitality assistant welcomes them while trying to finish another task. |
| 10–25 minutes | Help arrange a permitted reading place. Two ordinary candidates differ in light, shelter and proximity to the route people will soon use. Ask the assistant, inspect the places, or follow the attendant's work. The choice affects where the volume and its readers will be found later. |
| 25–40 minutes | Optional exploration offers a public walk, a view across different levels and a brief conversation with the attendant. Adso can ask about the building or simply share a joke about wet shoes. Nothing requires him to identify a planted clue. |
| 40–60 minutes | The canonical-hour transition begins. Work changes and a supervised threshold becomes unavailable. Adso's first impression is that guests have been refused. A player may qualify that immediately. William asks whether anyone refused them, separating social rejection from a closed door. The player chooses to ask about the rule or observe how supervision changes. |
| 60–75 minutes | Test the reading-place plan against the actual movement. A view of someone crossing a walk does not show which entrance they used. This small intellectual challenge can be resolved by walking, watching from another permitted position or asking a precise question. Revise the plan or keep it with an explicit limitation. |
| 75–90 minutes | The assistant uses the chosen arrangement. Adso reports what he established, and William accepts a local detail he had not witnessed. The attendant shares a warm seat or a small kindness without a quest. Then asks Adso, directly, to look tomorrow at a request recorded as both accepted and refused. A person and a question carry the invitation to continue. |

Do not force the human to make Adso's initial mistake. A tentative impression is sufficient, and players who reject it should receive different dialogue. No major crime is needed: access changes, a decision persists, an assumption is tested, someone becomes familiar and the next problem belongs to Adso rather than to William's task list.

## 9. Original-mission policy and content density

Use **four substantial original matters** across the proposed campaign, plus roughly five to seven short optional incidents. This is a starting budget, not a historical claim about what fits between source scenes. Review the actual scenario schedule before approving placement. If four investigations require Adso to seem absent from his defining relationship, reduce them.

Two substantial matters should be part of the shared experience; two should be optional. Optional cases provide relationships, alternate evidence opportunities and texture, never exclusive eligibility to finish the campaign. William may hear about a case, encounter one consequence or never learn every private detail. Residents may approach Adso after a specific experience of his care, rather than because he has become the house's designated detective.

Suitable categories include communication failures, ordinary custody, work coordination, conflicting recollections, manuscript descriptions and route or timing questions. A case needs a person with a stake, a test the player chooses and a proportionate outcome. Most should resolve their practical question while leaving relationships open.

Do not require every case to alter permissions or feed `[central pressure increases]`. An apology, corrected record or changed willingness to talk can be enough. At least one matter should remain wholly about ordinary life. Reject cases whose interest depends on an undisclosed source mechanism or whose purported originality is a renamed novel incident. Expansion beyond this stay is easier with original roles; Adso should not become an indefinitely reusable consulting detective.

## 10. The delegation: familiar knowledge becomes conditional

The delegation changes permissions, workloads, audiences and William's availability. Adso's memory becomes valuable precisely because it can now be outdated. Announce practical changes through activity, notices and people, then let players discover their implications. Do not silently invalidate all learned routines or grant the protagonist universal diplomatic access.

Three original examples show complementary knowledge:

1. **The invitation at the wrong entrance.** A visiting clerk interprets an unanswered message as a slight. William can explain why that interpretation matters and whom a clarification should reach. Adso knows that the ordinary carrier's route changed when a public room was reassigned. He establishes delivery and receipt separately. Together they can correct a practical misunderstanding without deciding any protected political result.
2. **The repeated account.** Several visitors report that a worker refused a request. William knows which authority issued the request and what its terms meant. Adso recognizes the local chain through which one account was repeated. He interviews the actual participant and tests access at the relevant interval. Multiple reports stop looking like multiple witnesses.
3. **The place for a conversation.** William identifies who needs to speak and the ordinary proprieties involved. Adso assesses current foot traffic, supervision and audibility in familiar public spaces. There are several workable arrangements with tradeoffs. Their choice changes privacy for this original conversation, not a major source event.

Adso should also help someone for reasons unrelated to William's side. A new clerk's uncertainty about a familiar route can become a reciprocal encounter. The delegation makes local competence socially consequential; it should not convert every servant into a clue source or every political issue into a delivery puzzle.

## 11. Adaptation philosophy

| Boundary | Policy |
|---|---|
| FIXED | Adso's named identity, travel with William, the mentor relationship, arrival at the abbey, the broad setting and approved intellectual/political circumstances |
| ADAPTABLE | Original daily-life scenes, ordinary conversations, investigation order, permitted exploration, minor fictional residents, approach, local consequences and notebook interpretation |
| PROTECTED / SOURCE-DEPENDENT | Major events, established outcomes, important revelations and the central mystery's solution; this report does not specify any of them |

A source-dependent passage requires separate adaptation review before interactive choices are authored around it. The rule is to preserve what it establishes while finding legitimate decisions around knowledge and local action. If no legitimate decision exists, do not decorate it with a fake branch.

Maintain separate internal records for content provenance and in-world reliability. Original fiction can contain a truthful witness; a document grounded in a historical practice can contain a mistaken fictional entry. Neither category certifies the assertion.

Marketing and onboarding should say this is an interactive adaptation with original scenes. Do not call inventions lost chapters. Compression must not fabricate canonical chronology. This spoiler-safe report and its original prototypes do not make a complete source adaptation automatically safe for an unfinished reader; broader playable material needs its own scope and review. No protected detail is necessary to decide the present identity question.

## 12. Audience and pitch clarity

“**Play as Adso and learn to investigate beside William**” is the clearest apprenticeship pitch. For unfamiliar players it needs one additional explanation: Adso is the young companion, and the player learns by testing what people say. Readers gain immediate recognition but may expect closely reproduced scenes or a familiar narrative voice. Declare the adaptation policy early.

“**Play an original assistant travelling with William**” communicates freedom well but raises the substitution question for readers. New players may barely distinguish it from B. Investigation players could prefer its wider consequences, while historical-game players need a clear working role rather than a literary justification.

“**Play a monastery clerk who meets William**” promises institutional knowledge, divided loyalty and practical responsibility. It may attract historical players most strongly, but needs more explanation of the encounter and could suggest administration rather than investigation.

These are positioning hypotheses. I would expect readers to favor B and players seeking unrestricted role authorship to favor C or D. Players unfamiliar with the book should favor whichever prototype gives them the clearest purpose and most satisfying test. If Adso requires prior literary affection to work, the choice has failed commercially as well as mechanically.

## 13. Concrete production consequences

The current documentation describes a bounded native proof, not a campaign, sustained mentorship or full actor simulation. Its discovery records do not yet express disputed testimony as required here. Adso's identity does not remove that shared investigation work.

| Area | Additional work for Adapted Adso |
|---|---|
| Writing | Establish a concise voice range; write acknowledgments for cautious, confident, mistaken and already-competent responses; keep player questions plausible for the role |
| Character design | Define Adso's visible hands, sleeves and presentation; design William as a recurring person whose physical behavior communicates attention and uncertainty |
| Animation | Support walking exchanges, reading, shared inspection and listening; avoid staging every report as two stationary speakers; reuse existing assets only after suitability review |
| William interactions | Author responses to evidence quality and missing local knowledge, plus visits with no investigation purpose; ensure he cannot supply a conclusion from unseen evidence |
| Continuity | Track who encountered each claim, what was disclosed and which original local consequence persists; reconcile compression and optional scenes with protected facts |
| Localization | Preserve source attribution, time scope, uncertainty and humor; translate necessary terminology naturally; review meanings rather than literal category names alone |
| Adaptation and spoiler review | Check invented characterization and scene placement; keep protected research out of spoiler-safe review copies and player-facing summaries |
| Historical review | Validate selected practices, duties, terminology and material tests; do not infer historical truth from an internally coherent clock or plausible dialogue |
| Scenario authoring and QA | Establish fixed event, access and custody accounts; test alternate evidence orders, missed windows, changed routines, unsupported reports and revisions |

Adso simplifies role explanation, the arrival learning curve and the reason for a personal notebook. He reduces the need for background selection, a customizable protagonist or several incompatible introductions. First-person presentation limits some avatar work, though it does not make character performance free.

He increases narrative QA substantially because every branch carries a characterization and continuity obligation in addition to logical correctness. This can become enormous if the team makes protected events broadly interactive. Bound original outcomes locally, avoid global approval meters and reconverge scenes while preserving their specific consequence. Compared with C, the extra burden is mainly review and reactive writing; compared with A, B reduces exact choreography but still owes source coherence.

## WHY PLAYING ADSO MAY BE A MISTAKE

1. **The mechanics do not need him.** Routes, testimony and hours work with any competent protagonist. Literary identity may add cost without improving the decisions.
2. **William may remain the owner of every question.** A gradual mentorship ladder can conceal the same subordinate agency for the entire campaign.
3. **The named role may inhibit experimentation.** Readers could avoid a reasonable choice because they fear “Adso would not say that.”
4. **New players may find him constraining without compensation.** Recognition has no value to someone who wants to define their own investigator.
5. **Fast learners may resent scripted ignorance.** Forcing early errors would make the learning arc incompatible with actual player competence.
6. **An adaptation disclaimer may satisfy neither audience.** Readers may reject invention while investigation players still feel limited by protected facts.
7. **Local agency can seem trivial beside central pressure.** Correcting a request may feel inconsequential if the game advertises control over a larger mystery.
8. **Growth may live mostly in prose.** More careful notebook wording is insufficient if tests, opportunities and consequences remain identical.
9. **The character voice can turn into pastiche.** Excessive literary reflection may replace a person with imitation narration and slow every action.
10. **Spoiler-safe approval becomes harder.** Characterization and placement may depend on material the owner cannot yet review directly.
11. **Expansion can distort the identity.** A long sequence of original cases risks making Adso an implausible full-time investigator.
12. **Memory may become an accessibility tax.** If competence means recalling every incidental route after a week away, the progression rewards retention more than reasoning.

## WHY NOT PLAYING ADSO MAY BE A MISTAKE

1. **We may reject him for the wrong reason.** Fixed literary facts do not prevent player-owned tests or conclusions; most authored investigations also have fixed pasts.
2. **An original companion can be a disguised substitute.** If their arc, station and William relationship duplicate Adso's function, the change solves less than claimed.
3. **The strongest pitch loses specificity.** “Original helper” needs explanation where Adso and William immediately establish the intended relationship for readers.
4. **The arrival learning curve loses its clearest alignment.** A local role can know too much; a newly invented novice needs that ignorance justified again.
5. **The notebook becomes less personal.** It may feel like a professional case interface rather than a trace of a young person's developing judgment.
6. **Removing Adso can weaken literary coherence.** Retaining William while replacing the companion invites its own adaptation questions and reader disappointment.
7. **More freedom can encourage an unfocused design.** Invented background branches and dramatic outcomes could displace the bounded institutional reasoning that makes the abbey useful.
8. **We may surrender a distinctive emotional payoff.** William recognizing the player's developed judgment could mean more when the player understands whose apprenticeship they are inhabiting.

## VERDICT

**ADAPTED ADSO**

## WHY

Adso gives the game's learning curve a recognizable human subject and makes the changing relationship with William part of progression. Strict chronology costs too much agency; an original companion is more flexible but offers no inherent mechanical advantage. Choose Adso with real local consequences and explicit adaptation boundaries.

## PLAYER FANTASY

You arrive beside a mind you admire and gradually become someone whose own experience deserves to be heard. You learn the abbey through ordinary encounters, test what others claim and accept responsibility for what you can honestly establish. Independence means contributing a different kind of knowledge, not replacing William.

## PROGRESSION

Routes become connected; bells become anticipated transitions; residents become individuals with bounded expertise. Vocabulary clarifies questions, observation becomes narrower, sources become traceable and confidence becomes proportionate. Relationships permit specific new opportunities. The player improves through choosing and revising tests, while William increasingly treats their account as a contribution.

## WILLIAM RELATIONSHIP

**Beginning →** He challenges the step between perception and assumption while leaving the test open.

**Middle →** He asks what Adso thinks the question is and changes a local plan when persuaded.

**End of the playable progression →** He exchanges incomplete information with Adso and cites his evidence. The player can address residents without routing conclusions through the teacher.

## FIRST 90 MINUTES

Arrive together; meet two working residents; choose an ordinary reading place. Explore freely, share a human moment, experience an office transition and question an apparent refusal. Test a route or sightline, revise the arrangement and see it used. End with a resident asking Adso directly about a small conflicting record.

## PLAYER AGENCY RULE

Past facts stay stable. Attention, order, tests, supported interpretations, confidence, selected disclosures and original local outcomes can change. Protected source outcomes cannot. Never offer a branch whose advertised result is impossible. Player understanding must affect action and another person's response, rather than remain private commentary.

## ADAPTATION RULE

Declare an interactive adaptation with original scenes. Preserve identity, relationship and recognizable foundations. Review major source material separately. Keep invented scenes distinct from canonical evidence, and do not assert that they occupied undocumented gaps in the novel. Original prototypes require no hidden source knowledge.

## ORIGINAL-MISSION POLICY

Begin with four substantial original matters, two optional, and five to seven optional short incidents. Favor communication, ordinary custody, records, timing and work coordination. Use specific local consequences. Some matters remain independent of central pressure. William need not know everything; residents may seek Adso after an earned encounter.

## PAPAL-DELEGATION FUNCTION

New authority changes access, routine and information audiences while occupying William. Adso knows lived local conditions; William understands formal context. Their accounts become complementary. Familiarity generates testable expectations and must be revised where conditions changed. Political pressure increases the consequence of precision without granting Adso control over protected outcomes.

## CAMPAIGN LENGTH

**8–10 hours for the main path; 10–12 with optional exploration**, across one continuous multi-day stay. These are production targets, not novel chronology. Keep meaningful unassigned time and avoid simulating every hour. Include source-dependent material only within a reviewed adaptation plan; compress content rather than adding repetitive cases to reach the target.

## THREE PROTOTYPES

Compare Adso with an original travelling companion using equal writing effort, equivalent evidence and comparable choice breadth. Start on paper with a plan, hour cards and scripted conversations. Use parallel original problems and counterbalance order to avoid replay knowledge. Include both readers and newcomers. No implementation is proposed for this round.

1. **The sentence worth correcting — mentorship, 20–25 minutes.** Two accounts of an ordinary doorway appear incompatible. The player selects a test and revises a sourced note; William responds to the specific reasoning. Required: attributed sources, two tests, revision and conditional dialogue. Adso matters because correction should feel like apprenticeship rather than an examiner grading a clerk. Validate if players defend or revise their account willingly and describe William's response as recognition. Abandon Adso if they chiefly seek his approval, or the original role makes the same exchange feel more self-directed without losing attachment.

2. **The familiar walk — initiative, two short linked sessions.** An ordinary arrangement learned earlier has changed. Nobody announces a problem. The player may notice, ask what changed and help an original resident independently. Required: persistent arrangement, repeatable routine, current observation and a missed-detail alternative. Adso matters when a modest act of initiative feels like growth in someone the player inhabits. Validate if familiarity changes the chosen test and players claim authorship of noticing. Abandon Adso if initiative feels out of character, or only a highlighted comparison makes players recognize their supposed development.

3. **A request while William is occupied — independence, 30–40 minutes.** A visiting clerk misreads a local delivery interruption. Adso can investigate and resolve the practical matter before exchanging formal context with William. Required: changed access, a bounded timetable, independent support paths, selective reporting and one persistent consequence. Adso matters if being trusted alone strengthens the relationship while allowing real action. Validate if players conclude without waiting and William learns something from their account. Abandon Adso if they feel stranded without their mentor, avoid acting because of the literary role, or prefer the original companion's broader authorship.

## BIGGEST RISK

**The apprenticeship remains a permission structure:** players wait for William to authorize questions and certify conclusions, even when the writing claims they have become independent.

## ABANDON ADSO IF...

After one focused revision, test with at least twelve newcomers and twelve readers. Switch to ORIGINAL COMPANION if two-thirds of either cohort prefer its authorship and cite Adso's identity constraints, rather than unequal writing or interface quality. Also abandon Adso if half still wait for William before concluding Prototype 3, despite understanding the evidence and having explicit authority to resolve the original local matter. Repeated difficulty making ordinary choices feel legitimate as Adso is evidence against the identity. Failure to remember the walk in both versions is evidence against the familiarity design and should be fixed in either model.

## GAME THESIS

An interactive adaptation in which Adso's development is the player's growing ability to understand a lived institution. Stable routes, recurring people and meaningful hours create expectations; investigation tests those expectations against sourced accounts. Original local consequences give decisions weight while protected literary facts retain their place. William's changing behavior makes growth visible, but the player owns the reasoning and the limits of the account.

## PLAYER PROMISE

“When you play this game, you will become Adso learning beside William, know an abbey through its ordinary life, test claims against places and hours you understand, and become capable of giving an account your teacher could not produce without you.”
