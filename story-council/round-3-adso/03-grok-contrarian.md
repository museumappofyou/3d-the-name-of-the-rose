# Story Council Round 3 — Contrarian Creative / Product Director

**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Seat:** Contrarian creative and product director. Assume both sides are wrong: the romance of playing Adso, and the blandness of an original protagonist invented to solve a design problem. The question is which version a normal player still cares about after forty minutes, and which pitch is distinctive rather than merely respectable.
**Spoiler policy:** The owner is still reading the novel. This report uses only the approved premises: Adso travels with William, they arrive at the abbey, William is an intellectual mentor, several days pass, disturbing and criminal matters come to matter more, a papal delegation or committee later changes the situation, and theological and political pressure rises. No deaths, culprits, solutions, hidden identities, late revelations, secret mechanisms, fates, motives, outcomes, twists, or spoiling chronology. Where specificity would leak plot, the phrase is `[central pressure increases]`. Nothing here is a missing chapter.

## The product fact under the literary argument

Players buy someone they want to be for an evening. They leave when the evening starts teaching them how to be that person.

"Play as Adso" is the clearest sentence this project has had, and the one most likely to sell reverent boredom. Round 2 feared a sidekick simulator and replaced him with a lay clerk or a borrowed writer. Those are how a meeting hides an uninvented character. Respectable is how the abbey becomes a museum with a famous guest.

The progression in this brief matches the repository: walked routes, heard bells, known faces, a sentence split into what was said and what was seen. Build that. Adso's name does not power it. His memoir judges it. Any ignorant arrival can learn a cloister. Only Adso can be scolded by a book the player may still be reading.

**Ship an original companion who arrives with William and is allowed to be quick and wrong. Do not ship Adso. Do not ship the tasteful clerk Round 2 invented in order to avoid shipping Adso.**

The name of that recommendation is **the sharp companion**. It is Model C with the passivity taken out.

## 1. Does playing Adso make the game better?

No. Playing the young man beside William is better than playing a functionary. Playing literary Adso makes that young man worse.

Adso adds the picture people already know: an old friar, a young companion, a winter abbey. That is the only free marketing asset. `GAME_DESIGN.md` set it aside for agency. Round 2 kept the relationship and deleted the famous boy. He also adds apprenticeship, which fits this abbey and does not require his name, his memoir, or his canonical innocence.

He costs the first hour. To feel like him, the player trusts a confident monk and lags the facts. To feel like a player, they catch something inside ten minutes. Readers punish a pupil who is too sharp. Investigation players punish one who is too slow. He also costs the ending. People who came to be him came for a story that cannot be authored while the owner is still reading it. Advertise that memoir and the game must spoil it or dodge it.

Readers will say Adso and mean the poster. After forty minutes they want to try something his story has already tried. Players who do not know the book want the picture, not the name. Historical players will tolerate a clerk. Investigation players, placed behind William on purpose, wait to become him. They will not.

**The version they care about after forty minutes is the young man on the road with William. The version they click is Adso. Lose the easier click.** "Play as Adso" sounds like every licensed walk-along. "Arrive with William until you can prove him incomplete" is a game people have not played. Audience-by-audience detail is in §11.

## 2. Can Adso have player agency?

He can have the agency of a reader with legs. He stops being the Adso readers mean as soon as he becomes a protagonist.

Safe even under a strict reading, if the major facts of his stay stay put:

- The order in which he looks at things William has already opened.
- Which ordinary details he writes.
- How long he lingers in a permitted place.
- Optional conversations that change nobody's fate.
- How confident the notebook sounds.
- Which of William's questions he pursues first, inside one afternoon.

That is real play for about an hour. It is a beautiful building with a choice of rooms. Players call it atmosphere while the rooms are new, and then they call it nothing.

Decisions that break the purchase:

- Loyalty that sits somewhere the book would have had to mention.
- An original relationship that matters more to him than William.
- Residents treating him as the person who solves the house.
- Him noticing the central disturbance before William, offered as canon.
- Any ending branch that rewrites what kind of man the week makes.
- Optional missions that would have been chapters, sold as things the memoir forgot.
- A first-day voice wittier than William, or as empirical as William. Readers feel the impersonation without a citation.

Adapted Adso tries to live between the lists. The gap is smaller than the pitch. The moment a resident brings Adso a problem William does not know, a reader asks why this is Adso. The moment no resident ever does, a player asks why they are holding the controller.

A fixed past with a movable use is the interesting rule, and it is not enough. If every reading gets the same next scene, the reading is a diary. Players forgive a fixed past. They do not forgive a fixed future that pretends their sentence was weighed.

For an original companion the facts stay put — who was in the yard, whether the door was barred — and the morning changes: who is ashamed, whether William sees the harsh wording, whether a clerk may blur it. For Adso that morning is the part the novel owns. Branching it sells a fan's fork under a fidelity poster. The smallest honest Adso agency is one public sentence and a face that can move two ways before the next office. The moment the sentence must match a later chapter, the face is no longer free. That smallest version is the whole game, and it does not need his name.

## 3. Adso as progression, without numbers

The brief is right about the shape and wrong about the bearer.

Place, time, people, method, and independence are one progression. The player starts unable to tell a normal door from a strange one. Later they can. The repository already has the honest half: a route counts if it was walked, a note can remember who said it, an office is a shared clock. What is missing is authorship.

**Hour 1.** The abbey is cold and socially sharp. Bells interrupt. Faces are costumes. If the hour is only awe, it is a trailer. Something petty and public has to be at risk first.

**Hour 3.** Two faces are people. One route can be re-walked without thinking. The player has treated a confident sentence as a fact and can name the mistake. They want the same bell again.

**Midpoint.** Memory makes them fast, and then someone uses the answer. Familiarity becomes a tool because a person wants a sentence, not because a ladder unlocks independence.

**Late.** The same corridors obey visitors. An ordinary door is shut. The player knows which loss is strange. William is busy with arguments they should not be quizzed on.

**Ending.** They leave a record shorter than their memory and know what they cut. No screen says the companion has qualified.

People explain themselves to a stranger and later assume knowledge: "you know the night round." Fluency is a pleasure. Contradictions stay gross. Re-walking always works. Scolding a missed hinge turns memory into a tax. William offers the next question early and later asks what the player has already decided. The notebook keeps the sloppy line struck beside the revision.

### Where the name makes the ladder worse

One early scene may have William suggest the test. By the end of day one the player should choose the test. By day two someone who is not William should be willing to talk. If "William suggests the test" lasts for hours because Adso is supposed to be young, the player is in the curriculum the last contrarian report already killed. The famous name sanctifies the curriculum. It does not shorten it.

Adso's canonical progress points at the man who will one day narrate the week. The game cannot show that destination without spoilers, and it cannot aim at it without the player sensing homework from the future. An original companion can become "the person who was in the corridor" and stop. That is a complete arc.

The real skill is negative: knowing which absences are ordinary. A player who has lived two days can see an empty stall and decline the clue. That is better than a detection bonus. Anyone who has lived two days can learn it.

## 4. William and the young man

If the player is Adso, the relationship becomes the product and the abbey becomes the set. That inverts the repository. The moat is the building as a witness. William is why a stranger agrees to learn the building. He becomes a problem when he is also the best scene in every hour.

They cross a yard and he asks what the player makes of a porter's delay. A bad answer gets an answer, not a grade. He is soon at a table the player does not share. Separation is the first agency. A day glued to mentorship means watching the character you would rather play, or playing the one you would rather watch.

Reunion compares what each could see. Once on the first day the player can correct him: a man he took for a monk is a lay worker. He is pleased. The miss is rank, not a scripted humiliation. He also treats a neat story as a smell when the player wants that story to spare someone. The argument is a sentence in front of a door. Theology may color a line. It is not the puzzle. Supper needs one dry remark with no clue in it. If every William scene is a lesson, players skip him, and the Adso pitch loses the relationship it was for.

What the young man becomes able to do: see who is missing and know whether the absence is the hour or the man; tell William how a servant moves, because servants keep talking to a boy and stop for the famous friar; withhold a true sentence that is too expensive to hand over. William shows this by stopping. He stops suggesting the test, stops finishing the account, and arrives after the answer has already mattered to someone less famous.

Played as Adso, that late competence asks the player to enjoy a status the book is not going to honor. The famous friar remains the center of the story they thought they bought.

## 5. Older Adso should not frame the game

The older voice explains, knows, and tells the player the hour is already interpreted. Investigation is the pleasure of not having that yet. Use no frame.

Sparse chapter lines look small enough to approve. They still install a second protagonist. One dawn sentence can leak an emotion the player has not earned. Reflection after a case steals the catch. The notebook may become a record only as the player's own struck lines and a fair copy they choose to shorten. No future man at the shoulder.

A labelled opt-in episode, for people who have finished the book, may use a frame later. It is a different product. Playing Adso makes the frame tempting. That is a reason to refuse the role.

## 6. The notebook

A pocket folio is enough. The writer does not need a famous future book. The player may always write, and may sort a line by hand:

- **Observation** — "I saw the choir door open before the second signal."
- **Testimony** — "The cantor says the door was shut."
- **Document** — "The slip names a different gathering."
- **Inference** — "He may mean the ordinary hour, not this one."
- **Uncertainty** — "I saw the door. I did not see who passed."

Early prose may settle a door because a brother said so. William asks whether the player saw it or heard him. The player moves the words. Categories are language from minute one, not a locked tree. The change is having been caught once. Old lines stay struck beside the revision. William never unlocks a tab. A tab called INFERENCE is a level in a habit.

A messy young man is not a worse interface. Fewer tools, taught as youth, is reverent boredom as UX. Mess lives in the sentences. The folio holds comparison and the final account. Living stays in the cloister.

## 7. Ninety minutes as Adso, good enough to show he is still the wrong product

This is **ORIGINAL FICTION** around the arrival. It is not a claim about the novel's first day.

They arrive together. The porter holds even William while a cart is argued. The player can watch the delay or try to help, and learn that help is not wanted from a boy.

A novice looks across the yard and looks away when a master turns. The cantor enjoys certainty. A bell. The player follows the wrong file, stands where guests do not, and hears the office from the wrong place. That place is information. The church is a social map.

The hosteller asks him to carry one of two similar bundles. He assumes the nearer one is the guest's. It was a kindness for a sick man. Small shame. No conspiracy.

The cantor says a boy left choir before the verse ended, and says it as fact. Literary Adso lets the certainty stand. William does not solve the walk. He asks whether a tone of voice is a place to stand. The player can time the door against the verse. If the player already doubted the cantor, they are ahead of Adso, and the scene has started to cosplay.

Between office and supper the cloister is walkable with no errand. Supper is a wrong custom and one quiet laugh. No clue in the bowl. If it cannot be slightly warm, cut it. William will be with the abbot when the boy is named: "Tell me only what you stood where to see." The player continues because a public shame is scheduled.

The hour needs the boy, the bell, and the bundle. It plays better if the young man is original and already too sure of the nearer bundle. Strict Adso is obliged to be the last person to notice his own assumption. Players do not enjoy being last, in a game they bought in order to notice.

## 8. Original missions under Adso's name

Safe material, all **ORIGINAL FICTION**: a misread bundle, a route the hosteller needs walked, a disputed moment at a door, a kindness read as negligence, a lamp count that disagrees with the lamps, two honest clocks. This abbey can carry those. Under Adso's name each one asks why the memoir omitted it. The player may not think the question. They feel it as soon as the side matter is more alive than the guarded story. Fun on the margin is the licensed-game failure: the original half is alive, the canon half is a corridor, and the brand makes the alive half feel unofficial.

Strict Adso can honestly hold zero original matters. The game is then a reenactment, which the design doc already described as passive.

Adapted Adso can hold three or four optional small matters in a whole stay, none on the critical path, none rewriting a famous relationship, each captioned as invention. That quota is too small to be the game and large enough to feel like filler. If William knows the matters, he dominates them. If he must not know, the narrator has a secret life, which is worse.

For an original companion the cap inverts. Original matters are the stay: about four substantial ones, a handful of minor ones, most known to William, at least one brought by a resident, at least one left unresolved. The title does not apologize and does not mention a memoir.

If a beat only works because the team knows what the book does next, it is unavailable. `[central pressure increases]` is authored from the public premises or it stays out.

## 9. The delegation

This is the best structural idea in Round 2. It changes the rules of a house the player has paid to learn. It is also where Adso is hardest to play.

Early, William is the door into rank and argument. The player lives lower: yards, novices, the wrong end of a meal. Round 2 was right about that split. Played as Adso, the split either follows the book, in which case the team is staging pages the owner cannot review, or it invents a local expertise the narration did not center, in which case players who know him feel the promotion.

Four **ORIGINAL FICTION** situations where both kinds of knowledge are required:

1. A visitor is promised silence after supper. William heard the rule at the table where rules are stated. The player has walked the cloister and knows the kitchen door still talks.
2. William knows the lodging order of the guests. The player knows which room shares a wall with the night stair, because he slept near the feet. Rank without the wall is etiquette. The wall without rank is gossip.
3. A session runs long and delays a bell. William, indoors, can describe the argument. The player, outside, sees who leaves choir because the hour slipped. Each half is useless alone.
4. A servant asks the companion, not William, where the porter really walks at night. People perform for the celebrated man. They speak differently to his boy.

Use two or three of these, not twelve. Politics without a face the player has eaten near is a faction tutorial. The player does not adjudicate the great argument.

## 10. If this report is overridden and Adso ships

Model B is not recommended. These are the minimum rules that keep it from lying.

**Fixed.** The player is Adso, young, arrived with William. William is sharper in argument and blind in ordinary rooms because of rank. The place is this abbey in a working winter. The public situation stands: days, inquiries, rising trouble, a delegation, pressure. The game never claims to be missing pages.

**Adaptable.** Conversations that are not famous scenes. The order of looking and asking inside a day. A very small number of optional original matters, labelled invention on the chapter card. What he writes about those matters. Exploration of public rooms the reconstruction already walks.

**Protected.** Major novel events are not the default campaign and are not "improved." Character outcomes are not branched. The novel's central mystery is not a puzzle to beat, lose, or rewrite. Any opt-in novel episode carries a spoiler label and a chapter range, and the original campaign does not depend on it.

**Marketing.** Store page, title card, and first loading line say **interactive adaptation**. They do not say "the story of the novel," "untold," or "what he really saw." If marketing cannot live with that sentence, the project cannot live with Adso.

The policy this seat actually wants is stricter: do not adapt the memoir. William may be William. The week in the book is a different story and is not running next door. Silence about Adso is cleaner than a title card that puts his name in the player's head.

## 11. Which pitch a stranger understands

| Pitch | What they hear | Who leans in | How they are disappointed |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Play as Adso and learn to investigate beside William." | A licensed story. I am the young one. He teaches me. The book happens. | Readers, film-aware players, gift buyers. | Rails, side quests, or a plot that never arrives. Readers police the voice. New players do not know why a good idea is out of character. |
| "Play an original assistant travelling with William." | A companion NPC was promoted. | Designers, until a good hour exists. Almost no one else. | Sounds like homework beside a cameo. A good hour was undersold. A tour was described accurately, and that is fatal. |
| "Play a monastery clerk who meets William." | A job, then a celebrity. | Historical and town-sim players. | The job is the game. William is a guest star. Arrival, the free relationship scene, never happens. |

As pure clarity, Adso wins, the clerk is second, and "assistant" is last. People do not daydream about assistance. Readers click Adso and set a slow tempo. Unfamiliar players need William and the road, not the name. Historical players judge the office and will not save a dull game because the boy is canonical. Investigation players care whether the cantor was caught fairly. Adso's innocence fights them. A clerk's audit bores them.

**The line for the page:** "Arrive with William. Learn one abbey until a confident man can be wrong."

Anger at minute ten, when the hour is clearly not the novel, is cheap. Anger at hour six is a review. A respectful Adso adaptation is crowded. A clerk anthology is easy to ignore. The distinctive game is a body cross-examining a sentence while the famous mind is sometimes the wrong instrument. Adso gets the stranger through the door and then forbids that half.

## 12. What Adso does to production

He simplifies the pitch meeting and complicates every pipeline after it.

**Writing and scenes.** Someone must write a beloved narrator without quoting him and without outshining William. The project has proved doors and chant paths. It has not proved twelve unfair lines for a cantor. Early hours are two-character blocking, so William's lines become the production line and get graded for pastiche. A film face sits in many heads. An original voice has to be good. It does not have to survive the book on the desk.

**Review.** Strict Adso imports a calendar the owner cannot be shown. Adapted Adso replaces it with arguments about whether an invention feels like him. Every scene grows three gates: adaptation, spoiler, and history, including a pass by someone who has finished the book and a separate owner-facing pass that does not leak. An original campaign still needs history and a lighter spoiler pass. It does not need fidelity to a future memoir.

**Systems.** Claims, routes, hours, and accounts are the same for any protagonist. Adso removes none of the engineering risk in `PLATFORMS.md`. The meeting he simplifies is over before the first playtest. Spend it on a want.

## Why playing Adso may be a mistake

1. **The poster mind is William.** Players who ask for Adso often want his intelligence. The hours land on the boy who does not have it yet.
2. **Innocence fights the first hour.** A faithful boy trusts the cantor. A player doubts him. One audience leaves feeling the character failed.
3. **Strict Adso cannot be authored here.** His days are the book, and the owner is still reading. A spine the funder cannot see will not be finished. Calling that "a later phase" lets it eat the schedule now.
4. **Adapted Adso is a false storefront.** The title sells a memoir. The design sells optional life around a protected plot. Players can tell which half was touchable.
5. **Original missions become apocrypha.** Three good inventions get called fan fiction. Fewer than that cannot fill a commercial length.
6. **The older voice will be requested.** Once he is Adso, someone asks for the memoir frame. The frame explains, and explanation kills the abbey's one rare mechanic.
7. **The buyers of the name do not replay.** They came to see what happens. Doors and chant paths do not bring them back. An original stay can at least vary whom you disappoint.
8. **Dialogue becomes a pastiche review.** Lines get graded against a book the team must not quote. Cautious William is a polite hint dispenser.
9. **The good progression becomes a course.** Place, time, people, method, and independence are excellent. Taught slowly because the pupil is canonical, they are a syllabus. Players wait to graduate into fun.
10. **Late competence reads as a cheat.** If Adso knows the night stair better than William, readers call it wrong. If he never does, the delegation loses its best turn.
11. **The novel's patience is the wrong tempo.** A day of awe is a chapter. The same day in a game is a quit, including among people who love the book.
12. **The name postpones the game.** "Be Adso" invites research. The slice needs one public shame and one walk.
13. **Familiarity replaces distinctiveness.** The thumbnail becomes a young monk and an old detective, which is already the idea of the book. The body testing a real door drops out of the promise, then out of the budget.

---

## Why refusing Adso may be a mistake

1. **The original is one bad sentence from nobody.** Clerk, notary, assistant, and borrowed writer solve access. None is a person anyone misses. If scene one cannot say what he wants from William, dropping Adso bought blandness.
2. **Recognition is not contemptible.** Play often starts because two people are already loved. Systems will not replace that. William alone can feel like luggage.
3. **The apprenticeship is a true fantasy.** People want a brilliant teacher and one moment of seeing past him. A camera-companion cannot do it. Refuse the name, and you still owe the arc.
4. **Another boy on the same road can feel like a demotion.** A title card will not argue readers in. If hour one is a tour plus a job, they were right to refuse.
5. **Split loyalty becomes a menu.** Tell William, tell the clerk, tell the boy. Adso's loyalty is simple, and simplicity is worth something.
6. **Spoiler fear sands off heat with the plot.** Comedy, embarrassment, and argument are not spoilers. An original game can still collapse into fair discrepancies in a lovely building. That is the Round 1 failure.
7. **The shared road is the onboarding.** A local helper assigned at the gate never becomes "we" with William. If that hour is dull, the famous name might have carried it. Prototype the pair before trusting the cut.
8. **A sharp original can be smug.** Clever from minute one, and never charged for it, he is a younger William. The correction has to cost a third person, slightly, in public.

## Three prototypes that can kill the name

Compare the name against a blank companion. Paper first: a cloister plan, an hours card, three people, a human William who may only ask questions. If the paper is hot, use the existing cloister and church. No new wing, no voice, no week. Run 1 and 3 before any new geometry. If 1 is dull under both names, the campaign is dull.

### Prototype 1 — The same door, two names

A cantor will name a frightened novice at chapter for leaving before a verse ended. William asks whether certainty is a place to stand. The player walks one door and says what the walk proves and what it does not. One group is Adso. The other has a blank name. **Systems:** one bell, one sightline, testimony kept as a quote. **Adso survives only if** his group acts as fast as the other and wants another scene more. **Abandon him if** they wait, apologize, call a guess out of character, or describe a prologue. If the groups feel the same, the name is a sticker.

### Prototype 2 — Notice without being sent

A dull errand passes something slightly wrong: outdoor boots in a stall, or a door barred early. No quest text. William is away and later asks an open question without mentioning it. Silence is survivable. **Adso survives only if** players raise it without asking whether he would have seen it. **Abandon him if** they stay quiet because "he wouldn't know," or because they expect the plot to announce itself. If the blank group is also silent, the verb failed. Fix that before any further argument about identity.

### Prototype 3 — The empty chair

William is in a meeting the player cannot enter. A servant is about to be blamed for leaving a night round the player already walked, for a dull reason, on the first evening. Memory, a re-walk, or a shrug are all allowed. William returns after the answer has been used. **Adso survives only if** the absence feels like trust. **Abandon him if** players treat the closed door as the hero having left. They came for William. Ship a shorter game about cross-examining the building, with him as a dangerous presence, rather than a long game about becoming worthy of him.

# Final recommendation

## VERDICT

**ANOTHER MODEL: THE SHARP COMPANION.**

An original young man who arrives on the road with William. He is not Adso, not a renamed Adso, and not the abbey's employee. He is quick, and he concludes too fast. The game is the cost of that speed inside a house that becomes familiar. Refuse strict Adso, adapted Adso, and the local assistant as the campaign. Refuse Round 2's clerk if the clerk is only a station.

## WHY

Adso is the clearest pitch and the worst tempo. His innocence fights the player's pride in the hour that decides whether they stay. His memoir fights every original matter that could fill the hours after. The progression in this brief is real and does not belong to him. An original clerk is sane to produce and empty to want. A local assistant has agency and no arrival. The sharp companion keeps the picture people want — the young man beside William — and gives him a playable fault that is the opposite of canonical delay. He is sure too soon. A walk proves him sloppy. Someone else feels it before the next bell.

## PLAYER FANTASY

You came with William because you wanted him to think you were worth the road, and you are afraid of going home ordinary. You notice things fast. On the first day you turn a notice into a certainty, and a person you can name pays a small public price for your neat story. You spend the next days learning the abbey until you can tell a quote from a door, a normal absence from a strange one, and a truth from a sentence someone powerful wants shortened. William is the standard you bruise yourself on. He is not the man you fetch for.

## PROGRESSION

Familiarity improves, and haste changes. The player comes to recognize routes, bells, duties, and who may be in a place. Early notes confuse testimony with fact. Later notes keep them apart, with the old line struck through and still readable. Confidence becomes a choice about how hard a sentence should be. Other people start talking to the player. William stops suggesting the test. The house grants access because it has seen them, then visitors rearrange rank and take some of it back. Nothing is numbered. Growth is a monk assuming you already know, and you declining a tempting oddity because yesterday's hour explains it.

## WILLIAM RELATIONSHIP

**Beginning:** they arrive together. He asks what the player makes of a real wait. He is amused by speed and unimpressed by neatness. He suggests a test once, in the first scene that needs it.

**Middle:** they are apart for long stretches. Reunions compare what each could see. He is wrong about one local fact and glad of the correction. He argues when the player wants his blessing on a sloppy account. Residents begin to bring ordinary trouble.

**End:** the delegation takes him into rooms the player does not enter. He asks what the house did while he was speaking. He can reject the wording and still be too late to stop it. The player can know who is missing and why that is ordinary, and can withhold a true detail William would have used. William's visible change is that he stops finishing the player's thoughts and starts arriving after they have mattered.

## FIRST 90 MINUTES

Arrive together. The porter makes William wait. The player forms a fast opinion of the delay and of a man in the yard. A bell. The player follows the wrong file, hears an office from the wrong place, and meets one novice and one cantor. The cantor is certain a boy left choir early. The tidy story is tempting to report. A bundle task gives the player a small, concrete mistake. William does not solve the choir. He asks what was seen, what was said, and what was only convenient. Supper is a human error with no clue in it. Before sleep he says he will be with the abbot when the boy is named, and that he will hear only what the player stood where to see. Continue because a shame is scheduled and quickness has already failed once.

## PLAYER AGENCY RULE

The past of a matter stays stable: doors, hours, custody, and what a body could perceive. What can change is the order of attention, what is written, what is said in public, whom the player helps when two people want the afternoon, what is withheld from William, and how hard or kind the fair copy is. Those choices alter the next morning's faces. They do not alter the novel, because the novel's events are not this campaign. A fixed fact with no social future is a diary. A branch that rewrites a canonical fate is out of bounds.

## ADAPTATION RULE

This is not an adaptation of Adso's stay. William and the abbey are recognizable. The public weather of the premises may be felt: days, inquiries, disturbance, a delegation, pressure. The game says once that the stay is original. It does not say "you are not Adso," and it does not mention him. Inventions are not marketed as recovered scenes. A later opt-in episode may touch the book for players who have finished it. It is labelled, optional, and unnecessary to this ending.

## ORIGINAL-MISSION POLICY

Original life and original matters are the whole campaign. About four substantial investigations and a handful of smaller human hours, across four days. Most are known to William. At least one is brought by a resident. At least one never feeds the delegation. Each substantial matter leaves a social mark, or an honest lack of one. Manuscripts are allowed and are not the default sport. No novel event is relabelled as original.

## PAPAL-DELEGATION FUNCTION

On the last day the same rooms change owners. Doors shut for rank. Meals move. People the player knows begin to perform. William is occupied with formal speech. The player knows routines, servants, and what the house did during the sessions. A few situations need both his reading and the player's lived one: a silence rule a kitchen door breaks, a lodging order that ignores a shared wall, a delayed bell that empties a stall the men indoors cannot see. The player does not settle the great argument. They decide whether a sentence about someone powerless is precise or survivable.

## CAMPAIGN LENGTH

Four in-game days and a short dawn, about five to seven hours. One public human stake on day one. William stops suggesting tests before day three is over. The delegation sits on day four, foreshadowed by preparation. If playtesters want more life, add a supper or a walk, not a fifth day. A week is how reverence makes room for boredom.

## THREE PROTOTYPES

1. **The same door, two names.** The same choir-door scene as Adso and as a blank companion. Adso survives only if his group acts as freely and wants to continue more. Waiting, apology, or "out of character" kills the role.
2. **Notice without being sent.** An oddity during a dull errand, William gone, no prompt. Adso survives only if players raise it without asking his memoir for permission. If neither group notices, fix the verb before arguing identity.
3. **The empty chair.** William is locked in a meeting. A servant needs the night round the player already walked. Adso survives only if the absence feels like trust. If players spend the scene trying to reach William, they came for him, and the boy is a delay.

## BIGGEST RISK

**Reverent boredom, purchased by a clear pitch.** Adso delivers it through fidelity, slow innocence, and side matters that feel unofficial. A tasteful clerk delivers it through duties, tours, and fair puzzles with nobody to miss. Either way the player understands the abbey and does not care who is ashamed at the next bell. The counter is a named person at risk before the first office ends, a protagonist who may be wrong immediately, and a kill on the campaign if that scene leaves a stranger unmoved.

## ABANDON ADSO IF...

Abandon him if Prototype 1's Adso group hesitates, apologizes, or polices itself more than the blank group. Abandon him if Prototype 2 stays silent because of what Adso would know. Abandon him if Prototype 3 treats William's closed door as the loss of the main character. Abandon him if the only gain from the name is a smile at the title and a description of the scene as a prologue. Any one result is enough. Do not average them into a compromise adaptation.

## GAME THESIS

This is a first-person game about being young, quick, and too sure, beside a brilliant friar, in one abbey whose doors, bells, and voices can prove a sentence false. You arrive with William. You learn the house as a body learns a house. People begin to trust your eyes. Power then arrives and wants those eyes for its own sentences. The pleasure is the moment you know the ordinary life of the place well enough to catch a convenient story, including your own, and the moment you decide who has to live with the wording.

## PLAYER PROMISE

When you play this game, you will arrive with William already sure of something, walk one abbey until that certainty fails in front of a real door and a real bell, and have to choose what a person you ate with is made to live with — while the brilliant man beside you is sometimes the one who does not know.
